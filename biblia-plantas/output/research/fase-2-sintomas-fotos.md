# Fase 2 — Síntomas abióticos/culturales, protocolo fotográfico y seguimiento

Fecha de consulta de todas las fuentes: 2026-10-21. Alcance: causas **abióticas y culturales** de 30 síntomas (S01-S30), cómo diferenciarlas, preguntas de confirmación, tratamiento escalonado, recuperación/descarte y urgencia. Las plagas y enfermedades aparecen solo como **puntero** (→ agentes de fitopatología/plagas). Las frases entre comillas son literales en el idioma original de la fuente; la traducción o glosa va fuera de las comillas. Los ids de fuente (A1…, B1…) remiten a la tabla final "## Fuentes". Nada de lo escrito contradice `output/logica/principios.md` §3.5 y §4.5; donde una fuente se aparta de lo fijado en Fase 1 (flúor y reposo del agua; pulverizar; profundidad del dedo; volúmenes de lavado) se anota en "## Contradicciones entre fuentes".

Convenciones de urgencia (criterio propio a partir de lo que dicen las fuentes): **Alta** = riesgo de muerte en días/semanas si no se actúa (pudrición basal/radicular, colapso, frío); **Media** = daño progresivo pero reversible si se corrige la causa; **Baja** = cosmético o fisiológico normal.

## A. Síntomas

### S01 Hojas amarillas generalizadas (toda la planta)

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UC IPM (tabla "Guide for Diagnosing Houseplant Problems", adaptada de Powell & Lindquist 1983) para "Foliage yellow-green generally throughout plant": "Excess light, overwatering, underwatering", "Insufficient fertilization", "High temperatures, especially when associated with dryness", "Insect infestation", "Poor root health from overwatering, excessive dryness, root disease". Y en el texto: "Weak growth or light green to yellow leaves may be due to nutrient deficiency (usually lack of nitrogen), root rot, exposure to too much or too little light, or sap-sucking insects and mites." [A1][A2]
- Penn State (clave de Moorman) "General yellowing of the plant": "Low fertility", "High temperature", "Light conditions too intense or insufficient", "Plant pot bound". [A19]
- Clemson HGIC 2251 "Yellowing of the Entire Plant": "Foliage may become yellow because of too little light, too little fertilizer, insect pests, or mites. Most often, yellowing is a symptom caused by overwatering. Check for drainage in the pot, examine the roots, and if most are dark, soft, and dead, it is better to discard the plant." [A17]
- UMD: "Leaf yellowing is often one of the first symptoms of plant stress." "**Overwatering is the number one reason** why indoor plants fail and will cause leaf yellowing." "If the stress-causing condition is not alleviated, yellow leaves may turn brown." "Noticing the pattern and progression of symptoms will help to diagnose the cause of the problem." "Many times plants exhibit symptoms that are attributed to insects or diseases when the real problem is in the plant's environment." [A4]
- RHS "Yellow leaves": "yellowing of leaves in large numbers may be caused by a wide range of factors; too much or too little water, overpotting (i.e. potting up into too large a container), too shady a position, too cold a temperature, a sudden drop in temperature or exposure to draughts." [A28]
- RHS (chequeo en 10 pasos): exceso de agua = "old and new leaves yellowing and dropping at the same time, browning in the middle of leaves, mouldy patches on leaves and flowers, stem rot and dark, mushy roots"; falta de agua = "older leaves at the base of a dry plant will often yellow and fall first". Corrientes: "draughts can cause leaf yellowing and dry leaf edges". [A29]
- Missouri Botanical Garden (guía visual): exceso de agua → "Plants may have dull, light green or yellowing leaves. Roots are brown and soft. The soil at the bottom of the pot may be wet and release bad odors."; poca luz → "light green foliage and begin to stretch or lean towards the light"; frío → "Tropical plants growing in extended periods of cold temperatures may lose vigor, be stunted, turn yellow and die." [A32]
- Arkansas (FSA6116) "Leaves Small and Off-Color": "usually associated with poor nutrition, but the same symptom can be caused by over watering, which kills roots and causes symptoms of poor nutrition." [A42]
- UConn: "Pale leaves and slow growth indicate a need for more frequent fertilization." Y en luz fuerte: "Sometimes a plant's leaves will pale noticeably or turn purplish while situated in strong light. This usually means that this particular plant has a lower light requirement". [A39]
- Illinois (Troubleshooting) "Causes of loss of normal foliage color": "Over-watering", "Lack of fertilizer", "Insect attack", "Improper light". [A37]
- Iowa State ordena la investigación "roughly most straightforward to address to hardest": 1 "Normal leaf shedding", 2 "Cold damage from transport", 3 "Temperature fluctuations" (corrientes de puertas, rejillas, ventanas; "Don't trap plants between the window and curtain"), 4 "Check watering practices", 5 "Check fertilizing practices. When plants are under-fertilized, they will often yellow and drop leaves", 6 "Adjust the humidity", 7 "Check light intensity". [A21]
- UF/IFAS (regla de simetría para separar nutrición de plaga/enfermedad): "biotic problems tend to appear with asymmetrical patterns, while nutrient deficiency symptoms tend to be distributed symmetrically over the plant tissue surface." [A43]
- Diferenciador clave por raíces (Illinois): "healthy plants generally have white roots... If the roots are rusty orange to light brown, it is underwatered, and if the roots are dark brown to black, it is overwatered." [A38]
- Punteros plaga/enfermedad: UC IPM "Injury from sap-sucking pests"; UMD tabla "Leaf yellowing, foliage fades, yellows browns or wilts" → áfidos, araña roja, cochinilla algodonosa, mosca blanca, cochinillas, pudrición radicular, "High soluble salts". [A1][A3]

**Preguntas de confirmación (implícitas en las fuentes)**: ¿cuándo regó por última vez y qué hay en el plato? (UMD: "Pour off any water that collects in saucers" [A5]); ¿está húmedo el sustrato a 2-3 cm? (ISU: "If it is dry down an inch or two, then water" [A22]); ¿ha sacado la planta del tiesto y de qué color son las raíces? (Illinois [A38], Clemson [A17], RHS "Tip the plant gently out of its pot and check the condition of the roots" [A28]); ¿es un tiesto mucho mayor que el cepellón? (RHS overpotting [A28]); ¿cambio de sitio, transporte en frío, corriente de puerta/ventana/radiador? (ISU [A21]); ¿cuándo abonó por última vez? (ISU [A21]); ¿son las viejas, las nuevas o todas? (UMD "pattern and progression" [A4]).

**Tratamiento (de menor a mayor agresividad)**
1. No abonar ni tratar a ciegas: "As soon as a leaf yellows... many run for a remedy... They may reach for fertilizer, or household pesticides, neither of which is an appropriate first response." (Illinois) [A38]
2. Revisar raíces y sustrato (sacar del tiesto) y ajustar riego: "If overwatering is the issue, withhold water until the soil is quite dry and gradually begin a new watering schedule." (Illinois) [A38]; RHS: "Check the plant roots to see if they appear too wet/dry (dark, soft, brittle roots indicate poor root health), and adjust watering accordingly." [A28]
3. Corregir entorno: alejar de corrientes y radiadores, dar más luz en invierno ("houseplants benefit from brighter positions, south facing windowsills for example, in winter", RHS) [A28].
4. Si la maceta es demasiado grande: "Repot into a smaller container if in too large a pot (i.e not filled by plant roots). Dry rootball with kitchen towel if it is soggy when repotting." (RHS) [A28]
5. Abonar solo tras descartar riego/luz: Illinois "After two weeks, apply a water-soluble, houseplant-specific fertilizer." [A38]
6. Trasplante a sustrato nuevo: Illinois "If no improvement is seen in the next two weeks, repot in fresh soil." [A38]
7. Podar lo irrecuperable, pero no lo verde: RHS "Cut off any mouldy, shrivelled, completely brown or completely yellow leaves... Retain leaves that are predominantly green until the plant has produced a fresh flush of leaves, or you could delay recovery." [A29]

**Recuperación y descarte**: Illinois da un calendario implícito: suspender riego → 2 semanas → abono → 2 semanas → trasplante [A38]; tras una mudanza "If the yellowing continues longer than a few weeks, place the plant in another spot" [A38]. RHS: "Many houseplants will recover quickly once their growing conditions are improved" [A29]. Descarte: Clemson, si la mayoría de las raíces están "dark, soft, and dead, it is better to discard the plant" [A17]; UC IPM: "it is sometimes not practical to restore a plant's health; it may be too late... it may be better to discard the plant and purchase a new one" [A2].

**Urgencia**: Media; **Alta** si hay raíces oscuras/blandas, base blanda u olor (ver S18).

**Fuentes**: A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, A17, A19, A21, A22, A28, A29, A32, A37, A38, A39, A42, A43.

### S02 Hojas amarillas solo las viejas (inferiores)

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UC IPM "Foliage yellow-green on older leaves": "Insufficient fertilizer, especially nitrogen", "Poor root health from pot-bound growth, compacted soil, poor drainage, overwatering", "Insufficient light", "Injury from sap-sucking pests such as whiteflies, soft scale insects, or spider mites". [A1]
- Penn State "Old leaves yellow": "Nitrogen, magnesium, or potassium (major elements) deficiency", "Overwatering", "Natural aging of leaves", "Plant pot bound", "Root rot". [A19]
- UF/IFAS (regla de movilidad): "deficiency symptoms of mobile nutrients will appear first in older parts of the plant... symptoms of N, P, K and Mg deficiencies will manifest in the old growth because they are mobile". [A43]
- MOBOT, deficiencia de nitrógeno: "The oldest leaves turn yellow at the margins and progress inward, which may lead to defoliation and reduced growth. When severe, only the new growth remains green while the rest of the foliage is yellow. An application of a fertilizer with nitrogen can give quick green-up effects." [A32]
- Arkansas "Slow Loss of Lower Leaves": "This problem usually indicates a low level of fertility. The plant is moving nutrients from the lowest (and oldest) leaves at the base of the plant to the growing points. Repot if needed and begin a fertilizer program." [A42]
- Clemson "Yellowing, Browning & Death of Lower Leaves": "This is generally caused by nitrogen or iron deficiency. Pot-bound plants are especially susceptible. Regular application of fertilizer and repotting periodically in fresh soil avoids this problem. Lower foliage may turn yellow and senesce if the plants are allowed to become too dry for an extended time." (Nota: Clemson incluye hierro en hojas viejas; contradice la regla de movilidad de UF/IFAS y Penn State → ver Contradicciones C1.) [A17]
- UMD (exceso de agua): "The primary symptom of excess moisture is wilting or yellowing of lower and inner leaves. If excess water continues, plants may show other drought symptoms, such as scorch, leaf drop, and/ or plant death." [A5]
- Senescencia normal: ISU "If only a few leaves are dropping and they are older inner leaves on a plant that otherwise looks very healthy, don't worry about it. Leaves don't live forever!" [A21]; RHS "It is normal for the odd lower leaf on a mature plant to yellow and fall" [A28]; RHS "Older leaves on healthy houseplants will naturally turn brown and die back over time as the plant matures, which is no cause for concern." [A28]; ISU suculentas "Brown lower leaves on otherwise healthy plants are normal" [A27].
- Falta de agua: RHS "older leaves at the base of a dry plant will often yellow and fall first" [A29]; WSU C064 "Tips of leaves become brown and leaves wilt. Lower leaves turn yellow and fall off" = "Not enough water" [A47].
- Poca luz: RHS "You might also see lower leaves turning yellow and falling" [A29]; MOBOT "Some plants will also drop older leaves" [A32].
- Raíces apelmazadas: Montana lista "Lower leaves are yellowing and falling off" entre los signos de que hace falta trasplantar [A52]; Clemson "Pot-bound plants are especially susceptible" [A17].
- Salinidad: Clemson (Indoor Plants) "Salt buildup in the soil can lead to root damage, causing symptoms such as reduced growth, brown leaf tips, dropping of lower leaves and wilting" [A18]; UMD "lower leaf drop" por toxicidad de abono [A6].

**Cómo diferenciar**: pocas hojas, interiores, planta sana → senescencia (ISU, RHS). Amarilleo que empieza en el margen de las más viejas y avanza hacia dentro con planta pálida y sin abono en meses → nitrógeno (MOBOT, Arkansas). Viejas e interiores amarillas con sustrato húmedo/plato con agua → exceso de agua (UMD). Viejas amarillas con sustrato seco y marchitez → sequía (RHS, WSU). Raíces que salen por el drenaje o agua que atraviesa el tiesto sin empaparlo → pot-bound (Montana, UMD [A10]). Patrón simétrico → nutricional; asimétrico → biótico (UF/IFAS).

**Preguntas**: ¿cuántas hojas y cuáles (solo las de abajo/interiores)? ¿cuándo abonó por última vez y cuánto lleva en el mismo sustrato? ¿sustrato seco o húmedo al tacto a 2-3 cm? ¿salen raíces por el drenaje? ¿el agua pasa de golpe sin absorberse? ¿costra blanca en sustrato o borde del tiesto (sales)? ¿hay luz suficiente (se inclina, entrenudos largos)?

**Tratamiento**
1. Si es senescencia: "Simply remove the leaf" (ISU suculentas) [A27].
2. Corregir riego según sustrato/raíces (ver S01).
3. Si hay sales: lavar el sustrato (ver S05) antes de abonar.
4. Si hay falta de abono: "begin a fertilizer program" (Arkansas) [A42]; UConn: "every 2 to 4 weeks when actively growing and less, if at all, during resting periods. It is better to use a quarter to half strength fertilizer solution more often than a full strength one"; "Never fertilize a dry plant. Water it first and then fertilize a day or so later." [A39]
5. Si hay raíces apelmazadas: trasplantar (UMD: "repotted into larger pots and the tight root mass should be loosened by hand or cut away") [A10].

**Recuperación y descarte**: MOBOT: el nitrógeno da "quick green-up effects" [A32] (sin plazo numérico). Las hojas ya amarillas no se recuperan (RHS: retirar solo las completamente amarillas) [A29]. Descarte: no aplica salvo pudrición radicular (S18).

**Urgencia**: Baja (senescencia) a Media.

**Fuentes**: A1, A5, A6, A10, A17, A18, A19, A21, A27, A28, A29, A32, A42, A43, A47, A52.

### S03 Hojas amarillas solo las nuevas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UC IPM "Foliage yellow-green on newer leaves": "Overwatering, soil pH imbalance", "Micronutrient deficiency". [A1]
- Penn State "Young leaves yellow": "Low light intensity", "Iron or manganese deficiency", "Overfertilization". [A19]
- UF/IFAS: "symptoms for immobile nutrients will be seen first in new growth... symptoms of Ca, Cu, B, Fe, Mn and Zn deficiencies will appear in the new growth because they are immobile." Y la disponibilidad depende del pH: "Fe, Mn, Zn and B exist in forms that are unavailable to plants at alkaline pH." [A43]
- MOBOT (hierro): "The classic symptom of iron deficiency is new leaves turning yellow but the veins remaining green. It is most prevalent on new, upper growth but eventually the whole plant may be affected. This is most common on plants that require an acid soil, such as azaleas." [A32]
- RHS: "If lime-hating plants (such as Gardenia) have upper leaves turning yellow but staying firm they may be suffering from excess calcium, and thus reduced availability of iron or manganese or both in the compost... or the prolonged use of hard water instead of rain water." [A28]
- Exceso de agua: RHS "old and new leaves yellowing and dropping at the same time" [A29]; UC IPM lo lista también en hojas nuevas [A1].
- Poca luz: ISU "Tall, spindly, lanky new growth that is pale in color is an indication that light levels are too low" (suculentas) [A27]; UConn "pale leaves, spindly stems" [A39].
- Luz excesiva (quemadura de brote nuevo): UMD "Damage appears as pale, bleached or faded areas" [A8]; ISU suculentas: quemadura "especially interior leaves or on new growth" [A27].

**Cómo diferenciar**: nuevas amarillas con nervios verdes y firmes, planta acidófila o regada con agua dura → clorosis férrica/manganeso (MOBOT, RHS, UMD S04). Nuevas pálidas, pequeñas y con entrenudos largos → luz (ISU, UConn). Nuevas y viejas a la vez con sustrato húmedo → exceso de agua (RHS). Nuevas blanqueadas tras un cambio a ventana soleada → quemadura (UMD, ISU). Nuevas quemadas en puntas con costra de sales → exceso de abono (Penn State).

**Preguntas**: ¿los nervios siguen verdes? ¿la hoja está firme o lacia? ¿agua del grifo (dureza, Madrid es agua blanda, ver Fase 1) o de lluvia? ¿especie acidófila (gardenia, azalea)? ¿cambio reciente a más sol? ¿abono reciente a dosis alta? ¿sustrato húmedo?

**Tratamiento**: ver S04 para clorosis; ver S19 para luz; ver S05 para sales; ver S01 para exceso de agua.

**Recuperación y descarte**: NO ENCONTRADO un plazo numérico en las fuentes abiertas. RHS indica que corrige con "foliar feed" y agua de lluvia [A28] (sin plazo).

**Urgencia**: Baja-Media.

**Fuentes**: A1, A8, A19, A27, A28, A29, A32, A39, A43.

### S04 Amarilleo entre nervios (clorosis internerval)

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UMD: "Iron chlorosis (leaf yellowing with green veins) is the most common nutrient deficiency. Acid-loving indoor plants like gardenias are prone to this." [A13]
- MOBOT: hierro, hojas nuevas, nervios verdes (cita en S03) [A32].
- RHS: "Interveinal yellowing in ericaceous plants (such as Gardenia, azalea) may also be due to lime-induced chlorosis caused by iron, magnesium or manganese being unavailable in non-ericaceous potting compost or induced by prolonged watering with hard tap water. Rain water, water from a dehumidifier or air-condition unit are ideal. However any form of root stress such as under or over watering can induce nutrient deficiencies. Over-feeding with potassium-rich fertiliser can induce magnesium deficiency." [A28]
- Penn State: "Young leaves yellow: ... Iron or manganese deficiency"; "Old leaves yellow: Nitrogen, magnesium, or potassium (major elements) deficiency". [A19]
- UF/IFAS: la clorosis internerval es uno de los patrones de la clave (Fig. 4 "A leaf exhibiting interveinal chorosis"); hojas viejas → nutrientes móviles (Mg entre ellos), hojas nuevas → inmóviles (Fe, Mn, Zn); "it may be difficult to identify specific plant nutrient deficiencies without plant tissue testing. This is because many deficiencies will present symptoms that look very similar to one another"; "Before fertilizers are applied to the soil, a soil test should be completed". [A43]
- Illinois (patrones): "Depending on what nutrient is needed, yellowing may begin on the outer edges of leaves, or from the vein outward, or the leaf may even curl." [A38]
- Abiótico no nutricional con el mismo dibujo: UMD "Leaf scorch... scorching symptoms can occur along leaf margins or between veins" por "over-fertilization, high soluble salts, lack of water" [A3]; UC IPM: "Root rot may sometimes be associated with high salt levels" [A1]; RHS: cualquier estrés radicular induce deficiencias [A28].
- Puntero: Penn State "Mosaic pattern of light and dark green: Virus infection, High temperature injury, Pesticide damage, Major element deficiency" [A19]; UMD "Leaf mottling: Viruses" [A3].

**Cómo diferenciar**: hojas **nuevas** con nervios verdes → Fe/Mn (inmóviles) (UF/IFAS, Penn State, MOBOT); hojas **viejas** con nervios verdes → Mg (móvil) (UF/IFAS, Penn State); patrón simétrico en toda la planta → nutricional; manchas irregulares/asimétricas o mosaico → virus/plaga (UF/IFAS, Penn State). Si además hay costra blanca o raíces dañadas → la deficiencia es inducida por sales o pudrición (UMD, UC IPM, RHS).

**Preguntas**: ¿en hojas nuevas o viejas? ¿especie acidófila? ¿agua dura del grifo durante meses? ¿abono reciente rico en potasio? ¿costra blanca? ¿raíces sanas? ¿sustrato sin cambiar en años (RHS: "Houseplants are often grown in the same pot for years and will quickly use up all the nutrients")? [A29]

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**
1. Descartar primero riego/sales/raíces: RHS "Consider whether other causes are responsible - drought, over-watering, pot-bound plant etc." [A28]
2. Agua de lluvia o de bajo contenido mineral: RHS [A28]; UMD "use a good water source low in dissolved minerals" [A7].
3. Abono foliar: RHS "Use a foliar feed (liquid fertiliser diluted and sprayed on leaves) to correct the problem." [A28]
4. Hierro quelado y acidificar: UMD "applying a chelated iron product according to the label directions and acidifying the soil using a sulphur product. Look for a product labeled for indoor plants or houseplants." [A13]
5. Trasplante a sustrato ácido: RHS "Repot using an ericaceous potting medium (if appropriate), removing as much old potting media as feasible." [A28]
6. Análisis de tejido/sustrato si persiste (UF/IFAS) [A43].

**Recuperación y descarte**: NO ENCONTRADO plazo numérico. Descarte solo si es virus (UMD: "No effective treatment, discard infected plants") [A3].

**Urgencia**: Baja-Media.

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A7, A13, A19, A28, A29, A32, A38, A43.

### S05 Puntas marrones

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UC IPM: "Brown leaf tips or leaf margins are usually caused by overfertilization, salt buildup, or excessively dry or wet soil, but they may also result from too much sun or heat through a window, low relative humidity, or a drafty setting." Tabla "Brown or scorched leaf tips": "Poor health from overwatering, excessive soil dryness (especially between waterings), excessive fertilizer or other soluble salts in the soil or water", "Specific nutrient toxicities (e.g., fluoride, copper, or boron)", "Low humidity", "Pesticide or mechanical injury". [A1]
- Iowa State (FAQ y guía): "The most likely cause is low humidity. The humidity level in homes is often low, especially during the winter months." "Inconsistent watering, in particular allowing plants to dry out too long between waterings, will cause leaf edges and tips to turn brown." "Excess salts can build up in the soil when too much fertilizer is applied, which can also lead to brown leaf tips." "Too much chlorine and other chemicals in the water, such as fluoride, can cause brown leaf tips, especially over an extended period of time on sensitive species like spider plant, ti plant, dracaena, prayer plant, and calathea. Use a different water source if this issue is suspected." ISU señala además que puede haber dos causas a la vez: "brown leaf tips may be due to low humidity and over-fertilizing. Address both to solve the problem." [A24][A21][A20]
- Clemson "Brown Leaf Tips & Edges": "can be caused by exposure to hot, dry air, improper watering, insect feeding, and salt accumulation. White to grey crusty deposits on the soil surface or rim of pots is a sign of salt buildup. (Do not confuse this with the white or yellow mold-like growth of saprophytic soil fungi.) Some houseplants are sensitive to fluoride or chlorine in tap water." [A17]
- UMD (toxicidad por abono): "The typical symptoms are browning or dieback of the leaf tips and margins. But can include reduced growth, lower leaf drop, dead root tips, and wilting." "Fertilizer salts may also accumulate on the surface of the potting media as a white crust, especially if bottom watered". "Salt deposits may be visible on the outsides of pots." [A6]
- UMD (pot-bound): "Leaves and stems will show tip dieback or marginal necrosis (browning) as a result of the lack of water." "Often when watering a pot-bound plant the water passes through the pot too quickly and isn't absorbed adequately so that the center of the root ball remains dry." [A10]
- Arkansas: "Over fertilization causes marginal or tip leaf burn on many plants, but that same symptom can also occur from under watering. A few plants, especially dracaenas and airplane plants, show tip burn on their leaves from high fluoride levels sometimes found in water or as a contaminant from potting soil." Agua dura/carbonatos: "When salt levels get high enough, the plant is unable to take up sufficient water and produces smaller leaves and sometimes leaves with marginal burn." [A42]
- MOBOT (salinidad): "At high levels, a typical reaction is for the tip or edge of leaves to turn yellow or brown and slowly die." MOBOT FAQ: "The browning of tips and/or margins of the leaves of indoor plants usually indicates unresolved stress... low humidity, excessive fertilizer or other soluble salts, poor water quality, unfavorable soil pH, overwatering, under watering, physical injury, pesticides, or direct sunlight." "Watering indoor plants with very cold water can also lead to difficulties with foliage". [A32][A33]
- RHS: "Brown tips or margins to the leaves may indicate over or more likely under-watering, which can be assessed by checking the roots. They may also be a sign of too dry an atmosphere, or poor ventilation... Over-watering often causes browning in the middle of the leaves." RHS (poorly): "Brown leaf tips and edges are a classic sign of low air humidity"; "especially over winter when the heating is on". [A28][A29]
- UConn: "Brown leaf tips often indicate not enough water. Do not let your plants get too dry." Y con calefacción de aire forzado "the plants can dry out pretty quickly even in the winter". [A39]
- Illinois: "Causes of browning of leaf tips: Improper watering, Exposure to cold drafts, Insect attack, Excess fertilizer"; "Brown-edged leaves may indicate an erratic watering schedule or exposure to cold drafts. Another cause... is an accumulation of fertilizer salts on containers." [A37][A38]
- OSU: "Burned leaf tips or a white crust on the soil surface usually signal too much fertilizer." [A41]
- Clemson (Indoor Plants): "Soils that have a white film on the surface or pots with a white crust on the rim or drainage hole may indicate that the plant is being overfertilized and/or possibly overwatered." [A18]
- Puntero enfermedad: Clemson anthracnosis "The leaf tips turn yellow, then tan, then dark brown. The browning may extend completely around the leaf." [A17]

**Cómo diferenciar**: punta marrón con **banda amarilla** y avance lento, planta con costra blanca en sustrato/tiesto → sales/abono (UMD, Clemson, MOBOT, OSU). Puntas marrones secas y crujientes en invierno con calefacción, sustrato correcto → humedad baja (ISU, RHS, UConn). Puntas marrones + hojas inferiores amarillas + sustrato que se separa del tiesto o seca en 1-2 días → sequía/pot-bound (WSU, UMD). Solo en especies sensibles (Chlorophytum, Dracaena, Cordyline, Maranta, Calathea) con agua de grifo → flúor/cloro (ISU, Arkansas). Puntas amarillas→tostadas→marrón oscuro que rodean la hoja → anthracnosis (puntero) (Clemson). Marrón **en el centro** de la hoja → exceso de agua (RHS).

**Preguntas**: ¿costra blanca en sustrato, borde del tiesto o agujero de drenaje? ¿riega por abajo o deja agua en el plato? ¿cuándo y cuánto abonó? ¿agua del grifo o de lluvia; especie sensible al flúor? ¿calefacción encendida/radiador cerca/corriente? ¿deja secar mucho entre riegos? ¿el agua atraviesa el tiesto de golpe? ¿hay insectos? ¿la punta tiene halo amarillo (UMD "yellow margins" en lesiones) o es seca sin halo?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor agresividad)**
1. Cosmético: "Trimming the brown portions off the leaves with scissors can temporarily improve appearance, but new browning will occur if the original issue is not addressed." (ISU) [A21]; Arkansas: "Dracaenas with tip-burned leaves can be reshaped with scissors" [A42].
2. Subir humedad real: ISU "Utilize a humidifier, pebble tray, terrarium, or group plants" [A21]; RHS "grouping plants together or placing them on trays of damp pebbles" [A29]; UMN: platos con bolas de arcilla y agua por debajo del nivel, agrupar, terrario [A51]. (Sobre pulverizar: ver Contradicciones C3.)
3. Riego regular y a fondo: UConn [A39]; UMD: regar hasta que salga por abajo; si el sustrato se separó del tiesto "may need several applications of water to rehydrate" [A5]; UMD (invierno): sumergir el tiesto hasta la mitad en agua tibia 1-2 h [A11].
4. Cambiar de agua en especies sensibles: ISU "Use a different water source" [A24]; Clemson "Use rainwater when possible" [A17].
5. Reducir abono: ISU "Flush soil, reduce fertilizer rates and frequency, and potentially repot" [A21]; "Do not apply fertilizer with every watering"; "use them at half or quarter strength"; "Fertilize only when plants are actively growing" [A22].
6. Lavar sales: UMD "Leach soil at least three times with pot volume of fresh water" [A3]; "Apply at least 3 times the volume of the pot of fresh water on the potting soil and let it drain completely" cada 4-6 meses [A7]; MOBOT "place pots in a sink and run water through the pot 3-4 times" [A32]; Clemson: "Drench plants with water periodically to leach some of the salts", "Empty the water from the saucer", "Remove salt crusts from the soil surface and add fresh soil" [A17]; UMD: "avoid bottom watering or pots without drain holes" [A7].
7. Trasplantar a sustrato nuevo: UMD "Smaller pots with media high in salts should have the growing media replaced" [A6]; Arkansas "Repotting with fresh potting soil is usually the best solution to the problem of burned leaves" [A42]; UMD: si hay costra en el exterior del tiesto, cambiar de tiesto o lavarlo con cepillo [A7].

**Recuperación y descarte**: la parte marrón no se recupera (ISU; RHS retirar solo lo totalmente marrón) [A21][A29]. NO ENCONTRADO plazo numérico de recuperación. Descarte: no aplica (Ask Extension/UMD lo califica de "aesthetic issue, but not really one of long-term plant health" para la toxicidad por flúor en Dracaena) [A54].

**Urgencia**: Baja; Media si hay sales con marchitez y caída de hojas inferiores (daño radicular, Clemson [A18]).

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A5, A6, A7, A10, A11, A17, A18, A20, A21, A22, A24, A28, A29, A32, A33, A37, A38, A39, A41, A42, A51, A54.

### S06 Bordes marrones (márgenes)

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**: las fuentes tratan puntas y márgenes juntos (UC IPM "Brown leaf tips or leaf margins"; UMD "leaf tips and margins"; Clemson "Brown Leaf Tips & Edges"; Arkansas "Marginal or Tip Leaf Burn"; Penn State "Leaf tips and margins dead: Overfertilization, Underwatering, Pesticide damage, Vascular wilt disease") [A1][A6][A17][A42][A19]. Matices propios del margen:
- Humedad baja: WSU C064 "Leaf edges are crinkly and brown" = "Lack of humidity" [A47]; RHS "crispy leaf edges" por falta de agua y por aire seco [A29].
- Frío/corrientes: RHS "draughts can cause leaf yellowing and dry leaf edges"; cortinas cerradas por la noche atrapan aire frío contra el cristal [A29]; Illinois "Exposure to cold drafts" [A37]; ISU "Don't trap plants between the window and curtain" [A21].
- Calor directo: RHS "Plants kept near direct sources of heat, like radiators, often suffer scorched leaves" [A29]; UC IPM "too much sun or heat through a window" [A1].
- Exceso de agua o de abono con bordes **negros**: UConn "Plants that are overwatered often exhibit black edges along the leaves, stems and petioles. Roots also blacken."; "If the edges of leaves and stems turn black and die, much the same as overwatering symptoms, excessive fertilizer use may be to blame." [A39]
- Pot-bound: UMD "marginal necrosis (browning)" [A10].
- Deficiencia de potasio: Penn State la lista como causa de amarilleo de hojas viejas [A19]; NO ENCONTRADO en las fuentes prioritarias abiertas una descripción específica de "borde quemado por potasio" en plantas de interior.
- Quemadura solar en variegadas: RHS "On variegated leaves, sun scorch is more likely on the paler sections of leaf" [A29].

**Cómo diferenciar**: borde seco y crujiente, uniforme en muchas hojas, invierno → aire seco (WSU, RHS); borde negro blando con sustrato húmedo → exceso de agua/abono (UConn); borde marrón en un lado de la planta, el orientado al cristal o al radiador → sol/calor (MOBOT "on the side of the plant directly aligned with the sun" [A32]; RHS); borde con halo amarillo y costra blanca → sales (S05); margen marrón + sustrato que seca en horas → pot-bound (UMD).

**Preguntas**: las de S05 más: ¿un solo lado de la planta (ventana/radiador)? ¿hojas contra el cristal o entre cortina y ventana? ¿bordes negros blandos o marrones secos?

**Tratamiento**: igual que S05; además alejar de cristal frío, radiador y rejillas (OSU: "Avoid placing leaves against cold glass and keep plants away from heating vents or fireplaces" [A41]); mover a sombra si hay quemadura (RHS "Move plant to a cooler or shadier position as required" [A28]).

**Recuperación y descarte**: igual que S05.

**Urgencia**: Baja; Media si bordes negros con sustrato húmedo (posible pudrición, ver S18).

**Fuentes**: A1, A6, A10, A17, A19, A21, A28, A29, A32, A37, A39, A41, A42, A47.

### S07 Manchas marrones secas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UC IPM "Leaf spots, blotches, blemishes, blisters, or scabby spots": "Excess light (sunburn) associated with a recent move of the plant", "Chilling injury (below 50°F)", "Chemical spray injury", "Overwatering", "Fungal or bacterial infections (rare unless plants have recently come from outdoors or greenhouses)". [A1]
- UMD (exceso de luz): "Strong sun and heat cause the breakdown of chlorophyll in the leaf. Damage appears as pale, bleached or faded areas. These areas eventually become brown and brittle." "Symptoms are more severe when strong sun is combined with dry soil conditions." "Sunburn may also occur on indoor plants that are adapted to low light conditions and moved directly outdoors without a 'hardening off' period." [A8]; tabla: "Bleached or whitened leaves: Excessive light or sunburn"; "Leaf spots and leaf blotches: Water spots, sunburn, various fungi, bacteria" [A3].
- MOBOT (quemadura): "Leaves or tissues turn brown or tan on the side of the plant directly aligned with the sun. This pattern is a good indicator of sunburn. In may cases the plant will still grow but may be stunted for a while." [A32]
- ISU (quemadura): "Leaf scorch is analogous to a 'sunburn' on the leaves. The damage is permanent and may lead to leaf drop"; "This issue often comes from a quick transition to a very different light intensity"; en suculentas "Brown or tan patches that form on leaves and/or stems, especially interior leaves or on new growth... The dead areas burned by the bright light will not recover". [A21][A27]
- RHS: "Strong, direct sunlight, such as from a south-facing window in summer, can cause brown scorched patches on leaves and give them a washed-out appearance." [A29]; "The upper leaves of shade-loving plants will often turn brown if they are placed in too much light." [A28]
- Manchas blancas/paja por agua fría o aerosoles: Clemson "Watering with cold water or splashing water on the leaves sometimes causes white or straw-colored spots or patches" [A17]; RHS "white or straw-coloured spots or rings are likely to be due to cold (i.e. not room temperature) water splashes, aerosol damage or watering with cold water. Pale coloured spots may also develop if plants have too much sun or from aerosol damage" [A28]; RHS "Pale spots are commonly caused by cleaning products and aerosols landing on leaves" [A29].
- Edema tardío (manchas corchosas): ver S13/S17; UMD "The bumps may become brown or necrotic and may be misdiagnosed as disease or insect feeding damage" [A12].
- Flúor/pesticidas: Penn State "Dead or yellow round spots on leaves (or elongated spots on linear-leaved plants): Fungal, bacterial, or virus infections; Fluoride toxicity; Pesticide damage". [A19]
- Illinois: "Leaf spots on foliage can be caused by too much sun. When spots appear consistently, move the plant to a place it receives less sun." "Causes of spotted foliage: Over-watering, Burning from direct sunlight, Disease". [A38][A37]
- Puntero enfermedad: Clemson, manchas fúngicas "small, brown spots with yellowish margins... concentric ring or target pattern. Small black dots (fruiting bodies of the fungi) may be visible in dead tissue" [A17]; UC IPM "tan to reddish brown to black roughly circular spots or lesions that may run together" [A1]; ISU "Brown to black spots on leaves that enlarge with age. Concentric circles form with dead, dark centers, often with fruiting structures" [A23].

**Cómo diferenciar**: mancha seca, papirácea, en el **lado del sol** o en las hojas superiores, tras cambio de sitio o llegada del verano a ventana sur/oeste, sin anillos ni puntos negros, no crece con los días → quemadura (MOBOT, UMD, ISU, RHS). Manchas blancas/paja, en la cara superior, tras regar con agua fría o usar abrillantador/aerosol → agua fría/aerosol (Clemson, RHS). Manchas redondas con anillos concéntricos, puntitos negros, que **crecen y se unen** → hongo (puntero). Simétricas → abiótico; asimétricas → biótico (UF/IFAS [A43]).

**Preguntas**: ¿cambió de sitio o sacó la planta fuera recientemente (UC IPM, ISU)? ¿en qué cara y lado de la planta? ¿usa abrillantador, limpiacristales o pulveriza con agua fría? ¿las manchas crecen o aparecen nuevas? ¿tienen anillos o puntos negros? ¿la planta venía de invernadero/exterior?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**
1. Quemadura: "Move the plant out of direct sunshine. Remove the damaged tissues." (MOBOT) [A32]; aclimatar: UMD "place plants in full sun for 2 hours per day initially and gradually increase" [A8]; ISU "Transition plants slowly to new light levels over several weeks" [A21].
2. Agua fría/aerosol: RHS "Avoid using cold water (allow it to become room temperature before use e.g for orchids). Avoid using aerosols (leaf shine products or insecticide products)" [A28]; ISU: no usar abrillantadores, limpiar con paño húmedo [A22].
3. Si parece hongo: Clemson "Remove and destroy infected plant material. Provide good air circulation... Avoid splashing water on the foliage" [A17]; UC IPM "Remove and discard infected leaves... watering early in the day" [A1]; RHS "Using fungicides on houseplants is not recommended" [A29] (puntero a fitopatología para fungicidas autorizados en España).

**Recuperación y descarte**: "The damage is permanent" (ISU) [A21]; "The dead areas burned by the bright light will not recover... Stems with sun scald damage will callus over creating a permanent scar" (ISU suculentas) [A27]; MOBOT: la planta "may be stunted for a while" [A32]. Descarte: no por quemadura.

**Urgencia**: Baja (quemadura/agua fría); Media si las manchas crecen (puntero enfermedad).

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A8, A12, A17, A19, A21, A22, A23, A27, A28, A29, A32, A37, A38, A43.

### S08 Manchas negras blandas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- Frío: RHS "Blackened leaves, leaf droop and whole plant collapse are symptoms of cold damage – commonly seen in unheated rooms, like porches, over winter." [A29]; UMD tabla "Leaf or shoot blackening: Cold Injury: symptoms may continue for up to a week after exposure. Protect plants from temperatures below 50° F." [A3]; Penn State "Leaves are very dark green with a water-soaked, limp appearance: Cold injury, Mechanical damage (crushing), Bacterial infection" [A19]; OSU (Sanchez): "Cold damage, for instance, might show up immediately in a very tender plant, or not for several days- when it might be harder to connect black spots on leaves to kids leaving a door or window open."; "African violets, jade plant, begonia, and more will all show damage if leaves are right up against a window on a cold night." [A40]; MOBOT: "Dropping, wilting or discolored leaves or spots on leaves can be signs of chilling injury... Wet soil and cold temperatures often lead to root rots." [A32]; UC IPM "Chilling injury (below 50°F)" [A1]; ISU "Cold damage from transport. Always box or bag plants anytime these tropical indoor plants are exposed to even a moments-worth of below-freezing temperatures." [A21]
- Exceso de agua/abono: UConn "Plants that are overwatered often exhibit black edges along the leaves, stems and petioles" [A39]; RHS "browning in the middle of leaves" por exceso de agua [A29].
- Puntero bacteriano: UC IPM "dark green, water-soaked spots that may turn tan, dark brown, or black with a yellow border... often visible when viewing from the underside of the leaf" [A1]; Clemson "water-soaked spots, sometimes with a yellow halo, usually uniform in size, and sometimes with a sticky ooze" [A17]; WSU "Water-soaked spots with yellow margins occur on leaves. Entire leaves may wilt and stems rot" [A47]; TAMU (suculentas) pudrición blanda "The diseased tissue will appear soft, watery, and black" [A53].

**Cómo diferenciar**: negro en hojas que tocaban el cristal, tras una noche fría, puerta abierta o transporte; aparece de golpe en muchas hojas y a veces días después → frío (RHS, UMD, OSU, MOBOT). Negro desde los bordes/pecíolos con sustrato empapado → exceso de agua/abono (UConn). Manchas acuosas, de tamaño uniforme, con halo amarillo, que exudan y se extienden → bacterias (puntero: Clemson, UC IPM). Con pudrición basal → S18.

**Preguntas**: ¿la planta estaba junto al cristal, en balcón/galería sin calefacción, o la transportó con frío en los últimos 7 días (UMD: hasta una semana de retraso)? ¿temperatura mínima de la habitación por la noche (UMD/UC IPM: <10 °C = daño)? ¿sustrato húmedo? ¿las manchas tienen halo amarillo y se extienden? ¿hay olor?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**
1. Frío: alejar del cristal y corrientes (OSU, ISU) y retirar el tejido dañado: MOBOT "If not severe, damaged foliage may be able to be removed and the plant will survive." [A32]
2. Reducir riego y revisar raíces si el sustrato está húmedo (MOBOT: frío + sustrato húmedo → pudrición) [A32].
3. Si parece bacteriano: UC IPM "lowering humidity, keeping plant surfaces dry, and prompt removal of infested plant parts. Clean hands with soap and water and disinfect pruning tools in 70% alcohol" [A1]; Clemson "Remove all diseased plant material. Avoid low temperatures, crowding plants, and spraying or splashing water onto the foliage" [A17].
4. Sistémico bacteriano: UC IPM "discarding infected plants is often the best approach" [A1].

**Recuperación y descarte**: UMD: los síntomas de frío pueden seguir apareciendo "up to a week" [A3]; MOBOT: sobrevive si no es grave [A32]; RHS: "whole plant collapse" en frío intenso [A29]. Descarte si colapso total o pudrición sistémica (UC IPM).

**Urgencia**: **Alta** (frío intenso o bacteriosis sistémica pueden matar la planta en días); Media si son pocas manchas sin halo ni progresión.

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A17, A19, A21, A29, A32, A39, A40, A47, A53.

### S09 Manchas con halo amarillo

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- Puntero principal (enfermedad): Clemson, hongos "small, brown spots with yellowish margins on the leaves. Spots may have a concentric ring or target pattern. Small black dots (fruiting bodies of the fungi) may be visible in dead tissue"; bacterias "water-soaked spots, sometimes with a yellow halo, usually uniform in size" [A17]; UC IPM bacterias "turn tan, dark brown, or black with a yellow border" [A1]; RHS "Dark spots with yellow margins on leaves may be due to fungal leaf spot" [A28]; RHS "dark spots with yellow margins suggests a fungal leaf spot" [A29]; WSU bacterial leaf spot [A47]; UMD tabla "Leaf spots and leaf blotches: Water spots, sunburn, various fungi, bacteria" [A3].
- Abiótico: Cornell (ejemplo fotográfico, no diagnóstico general) describe en una misma hoja "coppery sunken tissue in the center with no yellow margins and reddish-brown lesions with yellow margins moving in from the edge of the leaf" [B8]; UF/IFAS: simétrico = nutricional/abiótico, asimétrico = biótico [A43]. NO ENCONTRADO en las fuentes prioritarias abiertas una causa abiótica que produzca específicamente "halo amarillo" alrededor de una mancha, salvo la banda amarilla de transición de las puntas por sales (S05, MOBOT "turn yellow or brown and slowly die") y la quemadura descrita por UMD como "pale, bleached or faded areas... eventually become brown" [A8].

**Cómo diferenciar**: manchas con anillos concéntricos o puntitos negros, que crecen y se unen, en plantas pulverizadas o recién llegadas de invernadero/exterior → hongo (Clemson, ISU, UC IPM). Manchas acuosas, uniformes, con exudado, visibles mejor por el envés → bacteria (UC IPM, Clemson). Manchas secas sin halo, en el lado del sol → quemadura (S07). ISU: las enfermedades "are often secondary symptoms caused by poor environmental conditions and/or care"; causas "wet leaves (usually from misting or watering), poor air circulation, and/or overcrowding" [A23].

**Preguntas**: ¿pulveriza las hojas o las moja al regar (ISU, Clemson)? ¿la planta estuvo fuera o es nueva (UC IPM "rare unless plants have recently come from outdoors or greenhouses")? ¿las manchas crecen/aparecen nuevas cada semana? ¿hay anillos, puntos negros, exudado? ¿plantas apiñadas sin ventilación?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**
1. Retirar hojas afectadas y dejar de mojar el follaje: Clemson "Pick off and destroy infected leaves. Do not mist leaves." [A17]; UC IPM "Remove and discard infected leaves... as soon as disease symptoms are observed" [A1]; UMD "Remove spotted leaves and improve air circulation" [A3].
2. Entorno: ISU "Increase air circulation, Space plants..., Provide adequate drainage, Water using a wet-dry cycle, Raise light levels, Lower humidity" [A23].
3. Aislar: UC IPM "isolate the plant immediately to prevent the problem from spreading" [A1].
4. Fungicida solo preventivo y según etiqueta: ISU "most fungicides prevent the spread of disease issues but do not cure current infestations. Fungicides are not effective for controlling diseases caused by viruses or bacteria" [A23]; RHS "Using fungicides on houseplants is not recommended" [A29]; Clemson cita cobre jabón, clorotalonil, miclobutanil, tebuconazol, Bacillus amyloliquefaciens (productos EE. UU.; en España solo lo autorizado por MAPA → agente de fitopatología) [A17].
5. Descarte: ISU "It is better to eliminate the plant and prevent potential spread to nearby plants than deal with the disease" [A23].

**Recuperación y descarte**: NO ENCONTRADO plazo. Descarte si progresa pese a las medidas (ISU, UC IPM).

**Urgencia**: Media (riesgo de contagio a otras plantas); Alta si se extiende al tallo (UC IPM: "these lesions spread into the petioles and stems").

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A8, A17, A23, A28, A29, A43, A47, B8.

### S10 Moteado fino plateado

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- Puntero plaga (principal): UC IPM, araña roja "Feeding injury usually appears as light-colored spots or specks, called 'stipples' or 'stippling' damage"; trips "may cause stippling damage, similar to that caused by mites, but they also may deposit shiny black dots of excrement... Heavy thrips infestations on some plant species result in leaves that have silvery-gray damaged areas." [A1]; UMD "Fine stippling (numerous tiny dots caused by spider mite feeding)... webbing may be visible" [A3]; MOBOT trips "Flowers or leaves may develop silvery streaks. Heavily infested leaves appear brownish or silvery, and growing points may become contorted"; araña roja "stippled, yellowing and dirty leaves" [A32]; WSU "Leaves have fine webs and silvery spots" = araña roja [A47]; Arkansas "plants have a yellow/green-mottled appearance" (prueba: golpear sobre papel blanco, "you can see the dust-sized insects crawling across the page") [A42]; UMN "Check your plants by gently tapping leaves over a white piece of paper and look for orange specks" [A48]; OSU "their feeding leaves fine speckling that can bronze or yellow foliage" [A41].
- Abiótico con aspecto parecido: Penn State "Dead or yellow irregular spots or flecks: Thrips feeding, Air pollution, Pesticide damage, Cold water damage" [A19]; RHS "Pale coloured spots may also develop if plants have too much sun or from aerosol damage on exposed surfaces" [A28]; Clemson manchas blancas por agua fría [A17]; sales depositadas por riego por encima: RHS "overhead watering with (hard) tap water can leave noticeable calcium deposits" [A29]; UMD "Mineral and fertilizer salt deposits usually appear as white crystallized coatings on plant leaves" [A7].
- Contexto que favorece ácaros: UC IPM "The low humidity typically found in most indoor situations favors mite development. Also, dusty conditions... favor mite activity." [A1]; Arkansas "favored by warm, dry conditions... most injurious during the winter heating season" [A42]; MOBOT FAQ: sequedad y sales "can lead to explosive spider mite population increases" [A33].

**Cómo diferenciar**: moteado que aumenta semana a semana, con puntitos negros de excremento (trips) o telas finas y "polvo" que se mueve en el envés (ácaros) → plaga; moteado fijo, en la cara superior, tras pulverizar con agua fría/aerosol, que no progresa → daño por agua/aerosol; depósito blanco que se quita con un paño húmedo → cal.

**Preguntas**: ¿ha mirado el envés con lupa (UC IPM "A 10X hand lens is useful"; MSU 1987 "a 10X hand lens will be necessary" [A46])? ¿progresa? ¿hay puntos negros brillantes o telas? ¿se desprende al frotar? ¿pulveriza o usa aerosoles? ¿humedad baja y polvo?

**Tratamiento**: abiótico → dejar de pulverizar con agua fría/aerosoles y limpiar hojas con paño húmedo (RHS, ISU) [A28][A22]. Plaga → ver agentes de plagas; lo que dicen estas fuentes: UC IPM "frequently wash leaf surfaces with water, reduce dust, dispose of infested plant parts, and, as a last resort, apply horticultural oil or insecticidal soap... If a mite infestation persists or becomes worse, it may be best to discard the infested plant" [A1].

**Recuperación y descarte**: las hojas moteadas no recuperan el color (UC IPM: "leaves later bronzed and drying"); descarte si la plaga persiste (UC IPM).

**Urgencia**: Media (plaga de crecimiento rápido: UC IPM "At 75°F, mites develop from egg to adult in about two weeks").

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A7, A17, A19, A22, A28, A29, A32, A33, A41, A42, A46, A47, A48.

### S12 Masas algodonosas blancas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- Puntero plaga (cochinilla algodonosa): UC IPM "small, oval, whitish sap-sucking insects that cover houseplants with a cottony, powdery, or waxy material, often along the veins of leaves or where leafstalks join stems... may infest plant roots as well" [A1]; UMD "Fluffy white wax: Mealybugs... may occur on foliage, in leaf axles, leaf sheaths, roots, and bud scales" [A3]; MOBOT [A32]; ISU "Cottony masses, especially along the veins of leaves and where leaves and stems join" [A27]; Arkansas "White Globs on the Stems" [A42]; UMN "look like little fuzzy puffs and can produce a sticky substance called honeydew"; "They can also live on containers and other plant-related supplies" [A48]; TAMU: el exceso de agua "can attract insect pests, specifically mealybugs" [A53].
- Confusiones abióticas: edema reventado: RHS "The growths can rupture and have a white, powdery appearance or may become rust-coloured" [A30]; depósitos de cal/sales: UMD "white crystallized coatings on plant leaves" [A7], RHS "calcium deposits" [A29]; secreción natural: RHS "Some houseplants, such as Plumbago auriculata, produce a natural whitish deposit on the underside of the leaves" [A28]; moho gris (puntero enfermedad): RHS "A fuzzy, grey-brown covering on stems, leaves or flowers suggests grey mould" [A29]; oídio (S26).

**Cómo diferenciar**: masas en axilas, nervios y uniones de hoja-tallo, con melaza pegajosa, que al tocarlas revelan un insecto rosado → cochinilla; costra blanca fina y uniforme sobre el limbo que se disuelve con agua → cal; pústulas corchosas en el envés sin insecto → edema; pelusa gris sobre flores marchitas → Botrytis.

**Preguntas**: ¿dónde están (axilas, envés, raíces al sacar del tiesto)? ¿hojas o mesa pegajosas? ¿planta nueva o de otra colección (UC IPM: "Plant diseases are usually introduced in the nursery")? ¿sustrato muy húmedo (TAMU)?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**: UC IPM "scrape or wash them off plants with a spray of water, dispose of infested plant parts, and consider judicious use of horticultural oil or insecticidal soap"; alcohol isopropílico ≤70 % con bastoncillo, probando antes en una parte de la planta 1-2 días; "If the infestation is heavy or does not respond to treatments, then the best solution may be to dispose of the plant" [A1]; ISU: aislar, enjuagar, alcohol, "Multiple applications are often needed" [A27]; UMN: trasplantar con sustrato y tiesto limpios en plagas difíciles [A49]; OSU: podar partes infestadas, enjuagar envés [A41].

**Recuperación y descarte**: UC IPM "Control of mealybugs may be very difficult"; descarte si no responde.

**Urgencia**: Media-Alta por contagio.

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A7, A27, A28, A29, A30, A32, A41, A42, A48, A49, A53.

### S13 Escudos marrones en tallo u hojas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- Puntero plaga (cochinillas con escudo): UC IPM "small, brown or grayish, mostly stationary insects that have a waxy shell-like body or a hard protective covering... initially giving leaves a shiny, sticky surface"; "Scale insects can be difficult to detect on fern fronds because they resemble spore clusters" [A1]; UMD "raised black or brown bumps on leaves and stems" [A3]; MOBOT "white, brown, grey or reddish cushion-like to scaly bumps can be easily detected and removed. The canopy of leaves, table and floor may be sticky" [A32]; Arkansas "shaped like a turtle with a hard shell... often blend with the woody stems"; "The first notice that a pest is present is often the sticky goo on the floor" [A42]; OSU "some scales look like little brown bumps on the leaves, not resembling an insect at all" [A40].
- Confusión abiótica: edema (corky bumps): UMD "wart-like or crusty raised bumps or blisters on the lower leaf surfaces. The bumps may become brown or necrotic and may be misdiagnosed as disease or insect feeding damage" [A12]; MOBOT "It can be confused with scale or other insects or even disease" [A32]; RHS "rust-coloured and appear as scaly patches. These can sometimes be confused with other disorders or diseases" [A30]; Clemson "rough corky swellings form on the petioles and lower leaf surfaces" [A17]; Wisconsin "turning tan in color and corky in texture" [A35].

**Cómo diferenciar**: el escudo se desprende con la uña dejando la hoja intacta debajo y suele ir con melaza y hormigas (UC IPM, MOBOT, Arkansas); el edema está **dentro** del tejido (no se desprende), es corchoso, sobre todo en el **envés** de hojas viejas, en plantas regadas en exceso con poca luz y frío, sin melaza (Wisconsin, Clemson, UMD, RHS). En helechos, no confundir con soros (UC IPM).

**Preguntas**: ¿se desprende al rascar? ¿hay pegajosidad en hojas, mesa o suelo? ¿hormigas? ¿está en el envés, en pústulas, en especies propensas (UMD: "English ivy, Geranium, Schefflera, jade, snake plant, African violets, Pellionia, and aluminum plant")? ¿riego abundante en invierno con poca luz?

**Tratamiento**: cochinilla (puntero): UC IPM "scrape them off plants, wash crawling juvenile stages off plants with water, dispose of infested plant parts, and consider applying horticultural oil or insecticidal soap... most effective when scales are in the small immature nymph stage" [A1]; Illinois "If the plant is heavily infested with scale, it is best to discard it" [A38]. Edema: ver S17 (no quitar hojas: RHS "affected leaves should not be picked off as this will make the condition worse" [A30]).

**Urgencia**: Media (plaga) / Baja (edema).

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A12, A17, A30, A32, A35, A38, A40, A42.

### S14 Hojas caídas o lacias (marchitez)

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UC IPM: "Wilting is usually caused by underwatering or excessive light but may also be due to root decay from soil pathogens, often associated with overwatering or poor drainage. Inspect root systems whenever wilting occurs." Tabla "Leaf drop; sudden wilting of foliage": "Poor root health from overwatering, excessive dryness, root disease, excessive fertilizer, or other soluble salts in the soil or water", "Compacted soil or pot-bound roots", "Sudden change in light, temperature, or relative humidity", "Toxic chemical poured into soil". [A1]
- ISU: "Wilting is the result of roots not providing adequate moisture to the leaves and stems. It can be caused by two completely opposite reasons, either under-watering or over-watering, making it difficult to diagnose and solve." Sustrato seco por: no regar bastante, no regar a fondo, agua que "running around the dry root ball", tiesto demasiado pequeño, barro poroso, pot-bound. Sustrato húmedo por: riego frecuente, sin agujero de drenaje, plato/cubremacetas con agua, sustrato viejo compactado, tiesto demasiado grande. [A21][A26]
- Clemson: "Some common causes of wilting are a lack of water, excess water, root rot, too much fertilizer, and/or a salt buildup. Overwatering will cause the roots to die from a lack of oxygen... A pot-bound plant has filled its pot with roots. There is not enough soil to hold the water for all those roots, so the plant wilts... Fertilizer and minerals from hard water will accumulate in the soil and cause root damage and wilting." [A17]
- MOBOT: "Wilted leaves may indicate the soil is too dry OR too wet - rotting roots cannot take up water!" Sequía: "Wilting leaves, slight drooping or an off color can signal a plant is getting too dry... For small pots a quick and easy guide is to lift the pot. If it is light, it is in need of water." [A32]
- UMD (sequía): "Wilting for short periods of time does not harm plants. Sometimes a plant wilts on a hot day because moisture is evaporating from the leaves faster than the roots can supply it. If there is ample soil moisture, the plant will absorb water in the evening to firm up the stems and leaves." "Over a prolonged period, however, drought will cause serious damage" [A15]. UMD (pudrición): "Root rots may also paradoxically cause wilting even when the soil is wet" [A11][A14]. UMD tabla: "Plant wilting: Overwatering or lack of water... Healthy roots should be light-colored and firm." [A3]
- Penn State "Leaves wilted": "Overwatered (soggy soil; algae on soil surface)", "Underwatered (soil pulling away from the edge of the pot)", "Overfertilization", "Root rot", "Stem rot", "Plant not acclimated to low humidity", "Cold damage (leaves blacken and die)", "Plant pot bound". [A19]
- RHS: calor "Leaves wilting, rolling, bleaching and going crispy suggest a plant is suffering from heat stress"; frío "leaf droop"; "Overwatering can also cause wilting, and it's easy to mistake this for a lack of water and drown a plant, especially over winter when growth slows." "Too much light or sunshine in the middle of the day can cause leaves to droop". [A29][A28]
- UConn (ciclo vicioso): "When a plant is allowed to dry out the roots begin to die. The potting medium is then saturated with water with hopes that it will recover. Since some of the roots are no longer functional, the plant cannot take up this excess water... The plant will still look wilted and may receive more water, perpetuating the cycle." [A39]
- Puntero: UC IPM mosquitos del sustrato "Symptoms of infestation include sudden wilting" [A1]; UMD "Various root rots", "Stem cankers" [A3]; UC IPM bacterias sistémicas "wilting and general yellowing" [A1].

**Cómo diferenciar** (Penn State, ISU, UMD, MOBOT): sustrato seco, separado del tiesto, tiesto ligero → sequía; sustrato empapado, algas/moho en superficie, olor, raíces marrones blandas → exceso/pudrición; marchitez a mediodía con sustrato húmedo que se recupera por la noche → calor/luz (UMD, RHS); marchitez con hojas ennegrecidas → frío (Penn State, RHS); agua que atraviesa en segundos y planta que se marchita a diario → pot-bound (ISU, Clemson, UMD [A10]); marchitez tras abonar o con costra blanca → sales (Clemson, UC IPM).

**Preguntas**: ¿peso del tiesto (MOBOT, UMD "lift the pot")? ¿sustrato húmedo o seco a 2-3 cm y en el fondo (UMD palo/varilla hasta el fondo [A5])? ¿tiene agujero de drenaje y plato vacío? ¿cuándo regó? ¿se recupera por la noche? ¿abono o producto vertido recientemente? ¿sacó la planta y miró las raíces (UC IPM: siempre que haya marchitez)?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**
1. Sequía: regar a fondo hasta que drene (UMD, WSU); si el cepellón repele el agua, inmersión (UMD invierno: "placing the pot in a sink or bucket of warm tap water so that the pot is halfway submerged... for an hour or two" [A11]; MOBOT "once the soil in a pot gets very dry it may need to be soaked" [A32]).
2. Exceso: dejar secar, vaciar plato, comprobar drenaje (ISU, OSU "Water till the pot drains- then remove the excess!" [A40]).
3. Revisar raíces y podar las podridas: UConn "trim away the brown or black roots and repot in a container just large enough to accommodate the smaller root system... a more porous potting mix... Keep the newly repotted plant moist enough to prevent wilting" [A39]; UMD "cut out infested portions (rotten), and replant remaining healthy sections. Take cuttings and root them in sterile potting mix" [A3].
4. Pot-bound: trasplantar (Clemson, ISU).
5. Sales: lavar/trasplantar (Clemson "Repot in fresh soil") [A17].
6. Descartar: UMD "Severely damaged plants should be discarded" [A3]; OSU "There's very little to do once a plant gets root rot" [A41].

**Recuperación y descarte**: sequía leve: horas (UMD, "in the evening"); sequía prolongada: MOBOT "the plant may be on a spiraling course of decline and may not recover after the period of drought" [A32]; ISU suculentas: tras sequía severa "may not recover well because the root mass is not large enough" [A27]; pudrición: ISU "Plants rarely recover" [A23].

**Urgencia**: Media (sequía) / **Alta** (marchitez con sustrato húmedo = pudrición radicular probable).

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A5, A10, A11, A14, A15, A17, A19, A21, A23, A26, A27, A28, A29, A32, A39, A40, A41.

### S15 Hojas enrolladas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- RHS "Leaf deformation": "If kept in bright light in a dry atmosphere, leaves of certain plants (Maranta for example) curl inwards (concave). Cold and dry conditions may cause leaf margins to curl outwards (convex). Saintpaulia and Gloxinia are examples." Control: "Ensure plants are kept away from draughts or fluctuating temperatures. Water regularly... scrupulously avoid soggy potting media and bone-dry conditions". [A28]
- RHS (calor): "Leaves wilting, rolling, bleaching and going crispy suggest a plant is suffering from heat stress – common in very hot rooms like conservatories in mid-summer." [A29]
- WSU C064: "Leaves curl under; new leaves are undersized" = "Too much light. Give plant more shade or move it farther away from plant growing lights." "Stems become mushy... lower leaves curl and wilt; soil at top of pot is constantly wet" = "Too much water". [A47]
- UConn (humedad baja): "Leaves may also curl under and crack." [A39]
- Clemson (sequía crónica): "Chronic under-watering results in wilting, leaf curling, and death." [A17]; UMD "Plants wilt and leaves curl when roots are unable to supply sufficient moisture" [A15].
- Wisconsin (edema): "Some or all leaves may eventually shrivel or roll, and fall off." [A35]
- Punteros plaga: UC IPM ácaros ciclamen/anchos "thickened and brittle foliage, with stunted and downward-cupped leaf margins. Some of these symptoms are also characteristic of plant injury caused by chemicals, so infestations may be misidentified" [A1]; Arkansas "new leaves to be curled at the edges and twisted" (ácaros) [A42]; UC IPM áfidos "causing leaves to curl and become distorted" [A1]; MOBOT áfidos "twisted growth" [A32]; Penn State "Leaves crinkled: Natural habit of plant, Virus infection, Insect feeding" [A19].

**Cómo diferenciar**: enrollado hacia dentro (cóncavo) en hojas maduras con luz fuerte y aire seco → luz/humedad (RHS, WSU); márgenes hacia fuera en frío y seco → frío (RHS); enrollado con marchitez y sustrato seco → sequía (Clemson, UMD); enrollado de hojas bajas con sustrato siempre húmedo y tallo blando → exceso de agua (WSU); solo brotes nuevos, gruesos, quebradizos y deformes → ácaros/áfidos (puntero, UC IPM); todas las hojas así desde siempre → hábito natural (Penn State).

**Preguntas**: ¿hojas nuevas o viejas? ¿hacia dentro o hacia fuera? ¿sustrato seco/húmedo? ¿ventana soleada, calefacción, corriente fría? ¿hay insectos en los brotes?

**Tratamiento**: ajustar luz (sombra, WSU), humedad (S05), riego regular (RHS "checking one day a week and watering little and often in summer, maybe every fortnight in winter" como orientación, no como regla fija) [A28], alejar de corrientes (RHS). Plaga → agente de plagas (UC IPM: ácaros ciclamen "nearly impossible to see"; Arkansas: "Mites are almost impossible to control, so most people destroy infested plants").

**Recuperación y descarte**: NO ENCONTRADO plazo; las hojas deformadas no se enderezan (implícito en RHS "stop/start development").

**Urgencia**: Baja-Media; Alta si va con tallo blando (S18).

**Fuentes**: A1, A15, A17, A19, A28, A29, A32, A35, A39, A42, A47.

### S16 Hojas blandas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**

The soil surface will be wet or damp to the touch and when severe you will see mold or fungus gnats." [A27]; TAMU "One of the most common problems we find in samples that come into the lab is caused by overwatering" (suculentas) [A53]; RHS "This succulent has suffered stem rot and complete collapse due to overwatering" (pie de foto) y "stem rots on cacti and succulents, are signs the air is too moist" [A29]; OSU "The fleshier and more succulent a plant's leaves, the more likely it will suffer from 'wet feet'" [A40]; WSU "Stems become mushy, dark in color and rotten" [A47].
- Frío: Penn State "Leaves are very dark green with a water-soaked, limp appearance: Cold injury, Mechanical damage (crushing), Bacterial infection" [A19].
- Falta de agua (pérdida de turgencia): RHS "Wilted leaves, brown and crispy leaf edges... signs that a plant isn't getting enough water" [A29]; ISU suculentas: "Stems and leaves may become wrinkled and mottled" (ver S17) [A27].
- Puntero: pudrición blanda bacteriana (TAMU "soft, watery, and black"; UC IPM "soft and mushy... unpleasant odor") [A53][A1].

**Cómo diferenciar**: blandas, translúcidas o amarillentas con sustrato húmedo y base oscura → exceso de agua/pudrición; blandas y arrugadas con sustrato seco y tiesto ligero → sequía; blandas, oscuras y acuosas tras noche fría → frío; blandas, negras y con olor → bacteriosis (puntero).

**Preguntas**: ¿sustrato húmedo o seco? ¿cuándo regó? ¿tiene drenaje? ¿la base del tallo está firme? ¿temperatura nocturna/cristal? ¿olor?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**: ISU "Stop watering if over-watering is suspected. Allow the soil to dry out thoroughly between waterings. Make sure the container has a drainage hole... If soil is too organic or fine-textured, it will hold too much water. Repot with sharp-draining soil." [A27]; TAMU: regar solo con sustrato seco, empapando, con drenaje [A53]; si hay pudrición → S18 (esquejes de partes sanas).

**Recuperación y descarte**: ISU (pudrición en suculentas) y TAMU no dan plazo; ver S18.

**Urgencia**: **Alta** si el sustrato está húmedo (pudrición en marcha); Media si es sequía.

**Fuentes**: A1, A19, A27, A29, A40, A47, A53.

### S17 Hojas arrugadas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- Falta de agua (suculentas): ISU "When underwatered, leaves often turn yellow, shrivel, and brown before dropping off. Stems and leaves may become wrinkled and mottled. Roots die due to lack of water and then when succulents are finally watered again, may not recover well because the root mass is not large enough". [A27]
- Riego irregular en hojas en desarrollo: RHS "Where leaves are crinkled or creased it is usually due to stop/start development. This may be caused by variation in temperatures particularly from night to day, but is usually due to irregular watering when new leaves are developing." [A28]
- Exceso de sol / calor / aire seco: RHS "Shrivelled leaves on shade-loving plants are also a sign that the position is too sunny"; calor "going crispy"; humedad baja "shrivelled, crispy leaves with brown tips and edges". [A29]
- Edema avanzado: Wisconsin "Some or all leaves may eventually shrivel or roll" [A35].
- Puntero: Penn State "Leaves crinkled: Natural habit, Virus infection, Insect feeding"; "Leaves are very brittle: Minor nutrient deficiency, Mite feeding" [A19]; UC IPM ácaros "thickened and brittle foliage" [A1].

**Cómo diferenciar**: arrugas en hojas gruesas con sustrato seco y tiesto ligero → sequía (ISU); arrugas/pliegues solo en hojas que se formaron en un periodo concreto → riego irregular o salto térmico día/noche durante su desarrollo (RHS); arrugas + bordes crujientes en invierno → aire seco (RHS); arrugas en planta de sombra en ventana soleada → sol (RHS); pústulas corchosas en el envés previas → edema (Wisconsin).

**Preguntas**: ¿cuándo regó y cómo está el sustrato? ¿las hojas arrugadas son las que crecieron en un periodo concreto? ¿varía mucho la temperatura entre día y noche (ventana)? ¿sol directo? ¿pústulas en el envés?

**Tratamiento**: sequía → regar al secarse (ISU: "water when the soil is dry to the touch", prueba del palillo: "Insert a chopstick into the soil and if it comes out with moisture on it... the plant does not yet need to be irrigated" [A27]); riego regular y constante durante el crecimiento (RHS) [A28]; mover a menos sol / más humedad (RHS). Edema: Wisconsin "water less frequently in cloudy periods or under low light intensity. Water in the morning so that the soil... will drain by nightfall"; "Use a growth medium that drains well. Reduce relative humidity near leaf surfaces by increasing plant spacing and air circulation. Increase light and air temperatures"; "Empty standing water in saucers under pots 30 minutes after watering" [A35]; RHS: no arrancar las hojas afectadas [A30]; Clemson: "Leaves showing symptoms of oedema will not recover, but you can halt the decline of a plant by improving light and watering less often. Repotting to improve soil drainage may also help" [A17].

**Recuperación y descarte**: hojas arrugadas por riego irregular quedan así (RHS: desarrollo "stop/start"); edema "typically not fatal" (Wisconsin) [A35]; sequía severa en suculentas puede no recuperarse (ISU) [A27].

**Urgencia**: Baja-Media.

**Fuentes**: A1, A17, A19, A27, A28, A29, A30, A35.

### S18 Tallo blando o negro en la base

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UC IPM: "Soft stem bases usually are indicative of overwatering. Soggy soil can result from overwatering or poor drainage." Pudriciones por "water molds" (Pythium, Phytophthora): "Root rot is often associated with poor drainage and overwatering. Drainage can become poor as potting media ages, settles, loses aeration and porosity, and becomes compacted"; "Root rot may sometimes be associated with high salt levels". Bacterias sistémicas: "The cankers or rots will be soft and mushy in appearance and may have an unpleasant odor"; "most active under warm, damp conditions, on soft tissues, and in heavily fertilized plants". [A1]
- Clemson "Root Rot & Stem Rot": "leaves and stems show noticeable wilt. Stems may be girdled at or near soil level by a ring of brown or black tissue. Infected roots are brown to black and may be soft." [A17]
- UMD: Phytophthora "can also cause the lower portions of stems to turn black, brown or mushy which can lead to lower leaf yellowing and drop"; Pythium "The outer portion of the root can be easily pulled away from the core which leaves a fine hair-like thread. Often when a plant is pulled up from the potting media most of the root system is left behind in the pot"; Rhizoctonia "coarse reddish brown threads, often resembling spider webs, over the surface of infected stems and roots". [A14][A11]
- UMD (sales): "Fertilizer salts may also accumulate on the surface of the potting media as a white crust, especially if bottom watered, and cause stem collapse at the soil line." [A6]
- Penn State: "Stems rotted at the soil line: Fungal or bacterial disease, Overwatering"; "Stems rotted above the soil line: Fungal or bacterial rot, Sunburn". [A19]
- WSU: "Stems become mushy, dark in color and rotten, lower leaves curl and wilt; soil at top of pot is constantly wet" = "Too much water". [A47]
- MOBOT: "Root rot is most often the result when a plant is overwatered or the plant is grown in too large a pot or a pot with inadequate drainage." [A32]
- ISU: "Crown and/or Root Rot... Dark, mushy, limp, easily broken roots. Leaves change to yellow and blight (rapid death). Plants rarely recover. Caused by pathogens that thrive in wet or waterlogged soils, no drainage holes or saucers full of water, frequent watering, and/or poor soil quality." [A23]
- RHS: "Regular overwatering can lead to oedema and whole plant collapse"; "stem rots on cacti and succulents, are signs the air is too moist, which is common in bathrooms". [A29]
- TAMU (suculentas): "Soft Rot, caused by the bacterium Erwinia carotovora. The bacterium can enter plant tissue through natural openings and wounds. The diseased tissue will appear soft, watery, and black." [A53]

**Cómo diferenciar**: base blanda/negra con sustrato empapado, raíces marrones que se deshacen, olor → exceso de agua/pudrición (todas); anillo marrón en el cuello con marchitez → pudrición de cuello (Clemson); base colapsada con costra blanca y riego por abajo → sales (UMD); pudrición **por encima** del suelo en el lado del sol → quemadura (Penn State); tejido negro acuoso con olor que avanza rápido → bacteriosis (TAMU, UC IPM).

**Preguntas**: ¿tiene agujero de drenaje y cubremacetas con agua (ISU, OSU)? ¿con qué frecuencia riega y en qué estación (UMD invierno)? ¿tiesto muy grande para la planta (MOBOT, UMN)? ¿sustrato viejo/compactado (UC IPM)? ¿abono abundante (UC IPM bacterias)? ¿riega por abajo? ¿olor? ¿sacó la planta y comprobó si las raíces se quedan en el tiesto (UMD)?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor agresividad)**
1. Parar el riego, vaciar plato, asegurar drenaje (ISU, OSU, Clemson "Do not overwater the plants since too much water increases the occurrence of root rot") [A17].
2. Sacar del tiesto e inspeccionar: UC IPM "Healthy roots should be white, entirely so or at least under their outer covering"; "Off-color (brownish to blackish, or dark yellowish) roots, especially at the root tips, often indicate that root rot is present" [A1].
3. Cortar lo podrido y trasplantar en sustrato estéril a tiesto ajustado: Clemson "If only a few roots are infected, cut out these roots and repot the plant in sterile soil" [A17]; UMD "cut out infested portions (rotten), and replant remaining healthy sections" [A3]; UConn (ver S14) [A39]; MOBOT "plants with just a stem or small part of the plant with a rot, may be salvaged by pruning out the rotted part" [A32].
4. Esquejes de partes sanas: MOBOT "A plant with root rot may be a candidate for rooting stem cuttings of unaffected shoots" [A32]; UMD "only take stem cuttings from healthy tissue"; "Take cuttings and root them in sterile potting mix" [A14][A3].
5. Fungicidas: Clemson "Fungicides are available; however, most indoor gardeners will find that these chemicals probably cost more than a new plant" [A17]; bacterias sistémicas: UC IPM "Pesticide sprays are not effective" [A1].
6. Descartar: MOBOT "It is best to dispose of plants with root rots" [A32]; UMD "Throw away severely infected plants" [A14]; UC IPM "discarding infected plants is often the best approach" (bacterias) [A1]; OSU "Unless it's a well-beloved plant, it could be better to put up the white flag" [A41]. Prevención: UMD "The number one strategy for avoiding root rots has to be proper watering... only using clean or new pots... utilize a sterile potting media, remove and separate infected plants" [A11].

**Recuperación y descarte**: ISU "Plants rarely recover" [A23]; UMD: raíces "light-colored and firm" = sanas [A3]. Descartar si la base está ennegrecida en todo el perímetro o huele (criterio derivado de Clemson "girdled" y UC IPM "unpleasant odor").

**Urgencia**: **Alta** (pista del encargo confirmada por las fuentes: UC IPM "Soft stem bases usually are indicative of overwatering"; ISU "rapid death").

**Cómo diferenciar**: polvo que **crece** en colonias circulares, en el haz, en especies propensas y con aire estancado → oídio; depósito uniforme donde cayeron gotas, se quita con agua, no crece → cal/aerosol; motas en pústulas del envés → edema; masas algodonosas en axilas → cochinilla.

**Preguntas**: ¿pulveriza con agua del grifo (en Madrid el agua es blanda, ver Fase 1)? ¿usa abrillantador? ¿las manchas crecen? ¿plantas apiñadas, baño húmedo, cerca de ventana fría (UC IPM)?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**: limpiar con paño húmedo (ISU); dejar de pulverizar/aerosoles (RHS); oídio: retirar hojas, separar plantas, más luz, regar por la mañana (UC IPM, Clemson, ISU); fungicidas solo si grave y etiquetado para interior (UMD "Spray with a registered fungicide if disease is severe. Check horticultural oil labels" [A3]; RHS no recomienda fungicidas en plantas de interior [A29]).

**Recuperación y descarte**: UC IPM "usually will not kill a plant"; defoliación posible [A1].

**Urgencia**: Baja-Media.

**Fuentes**: A1, A3, A7, A17, A22, A23, A28, A29, A30, A32, A47.

### S27 Agujeros en hojas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- UMD tabla "Leaves eaten or chewed": "Check plants that have been outside for the summer for pests such as caterpillars, leaf-feeding beetles, weevils, grasshoppers, crickets, slugs, and earwigs. Handpick pests and repot plants before moving plants inside." [A3]
- UMD HGIC vía Ask Extension (2024): "The holes are minor with regards to plant health... nor would it be advisable to use an insecticide (not many are labeled for use indoors) without having identified the culprit. The pattern of chewing suggests either an adult beetle (possibly a weevil) or a caterpillar. Slug/snail chewing can look similar, but they would be less likely to climb that high on an indoor plant where the ambient humidity is lower... You could try inspecting the plant after dark... It's also possible the damage was done a while ago and the insect... is no longer present. Leaves cannot heal from damage, so it sometimes can be difficult to tell how fresh or old the damage is. Did the plant spend the summer outside? If so, the chewing probably took place there". [A54]
- Daño mecánico: RHS "Leaves can also be bruised by passing pets and people." [A28]
- Hongos que dejan tejido muerto que se desprende: Clemson "Sometimes the lesions run together, and the entire leaf dies" [A17] (no dice "agujeros" explícitamente → se anota como inferencia débil).
- Causa abiótica específica de agujeros (p. ej. rotura de hojas nuevas por humedad baja): **NO ENCONTRADO** en fuentes prioritarias.

**Cómo diferenciar**: bordes de agujero irregulares, mordidos, en planta que estuvo fuera o es nueva → masticador (UMD); agujeros con borde seco marrón en el centro de antiguas manchas → lesión fúngica caída (inferencia desde Clemson); desgarros lineales en hojas grandes en zonas de paso → mecánico (RHS); agujeros que no aumentan en semanas → daño antiguo (UMD Ask Extension).

**Preguntas**: ¿estuvo fuera o es nueva? ¿aparecen agujeros nuevos? ¿inspeccionó de noche y el envés? ¿mascotas/niños/zona de paso? ¿había manchas antes en ese sitio?

**Tratamiento**: UMD "Handpick pests and repot plants before moving plants inside" [A3]; Ask Extension/UMD: no insecticida sin identificar [A54]. Si no progresa: nada.

**Recuperación y descarte**: "Leaves cannot heal from damage" [A54].

**Urgencia**: Baja.

**Fuentes**: A3, A17, A28, A54.

### S28 Hojas pegajosas

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- Puntero plaga (melaza): UC IPM "Leaves covered with a sticky substance; sooty mold growing on leaves; small brown, white, or greenish objects seen on leaves or in crotches of branches... ants may be present: Scale, mealybug, or aphid infestation"; áfidos "excrete a shiny, sticky sap, called 'honeydew,' that can attract ants"; cochinillas "initially giving leaves a shiny, sticky surface" [A1]; Arkansas "The first notice that a pest is present is often the sticky goo on the floor, table or leaves" [A42]; MOBOT: cochinilla y áfidos "The canopy of leaves, table and floor may be sticky" [A32]; OSU "Avoid plants with sticky residue on leaves when purchasing: this 'honeydew' is a sign of aphid and whitefly problems" [A40]; UMN cochinilla algodonosa "honeydew" [A48]; WSU "Leaves have sticky deposits" = cochinillas [A47].
- Abiótico/fisiológico: Penn State "Leaves with a sticky substance on the surface: Material excreted by insects; Natural secretions of the plant (note if material is located in the same general area on each leaf)" [A19]; RHS "Secretions": "When houseplants are in active growth and warm daytime temperatures are followed by cold night temperatures, stress ruptures of cells may occur, releasing sugary exudates - this is known as guttation... Some plants produce sticky deposits on the leaves in response to cultural stress. Common examples include Cymbidium, Ctenanthe, Philodendron, Tibouchina and Zantedeschia. Such symptoms indicate poor growing conditions." Control: "Improve cultural conditions; avoid excess watering. Consider repotting the plant in spring. Regular feeding and watering should help" [A28]; abrillantadores: ISU "create a sticky layer that collects dust" [A22].

**Cómo diferenciar**: gotas pegajosas distribuidas al azar, también en mesa/suelo, con insectos o escudos en envés/axilas y a veces hormigas → melaza (UC IPM, Arkansas); gotas siempre en la **misma posición** de cada hoja (p. ej. márgenes, hidátodos), sin insectos, en especies citadas, con noches frías y días cálidos o riego abundante → gutación/secreción (Penn State, RHS); película uniforme tras aplicar producto → abrillantador (ISU).

**Preguntas**: ¿ha mirado envés y axilas con lupa? ¿hay hormigas? ¿las gotas están en el mismo punto en todas las hojas? ¿usó abrillantador? ¿riego abundante con noches frías?

**Tratamiento**: fisiológico → RHS (riego moderado, trasplante en primavera, abono regular) [A28]; abrillantador → limpiar con paño (ISU) [A22]; plaga → agente de plagas (UC IPM: lavar con agua, jabón/aceite, aislar; control de hormigas "If ants are found in potted plants, remove the containers from the building and discard them" [A1]).

**Recuperación y descarte**: NO ENCONTRADO plazo.

**Urgencia**: Baja (fisiológico) / Media (plaga, por contagio y fumagina).

**Fuentes**: A1, A19, A22, A28, A32, A40, A42, A47, A48.

### S29 Residuo negro (fumagina)

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**: síntoma secundario de plaga chupadora (puntero): Penn State "Leaves with black growth in patches in general over the surface: Sooty mold (look for aphid and scale insect activity)" [A19]; UC IPM "sooty mold growing on leaves" junto a melaza → cochinillas, cochinilla algodonosa, áfidos; áfidos "honeydew... encourage growth of sooty mold" [A1]; MSU 1987 "Aphids excrete honeydew, which can build up on leaves and promote growth of sooty mold" [A46]. ### S30 Raíces saliendo por el drenaje

**Causas y cómo diferenciarlas**
- WSU C064 "Pot too small": "Stems and leaves grow slowly even though plant is fed. Soil dries out quickly. Roots grow through drainage holes." → "Move plant into larger pot. Increase pot size by only one or two inches." [A47]
- Montana: signos de trasplante: "Roots are growing out of the bottom of the pot", "The plant is rootbound, roots are dense and tangled, without room for more growth", "The plant is top-heavy and wants to tip over", "It requires water too frequently", "There is salt/mineral buildup on soil and/or the pot", "The plant is sickly and not growing well", "Lower leaves are yellowing and falling off". Tiesto nuevo "about 1/3 wider and deeper than the previous pot"; "The optimal time of year to transplant is late winter/early spring... However, if a plant is showing signs of stress as listed above, repot it anytime." Pasos: humedecer el cepellón, sacar, "Trim off any dead, diseased, or damaged roots or roots growing out of the bottom", desenredar o hacer cortes de ~1,3 cm en raíces muy apelmazadas con cuchilla afilada y estéril, rellenar, regar y dejar drenar; después "Keep the newly repotted plant in indirect light for a few days"; "Do not fertilize for at least two weeks". [A52]
- UMN: "Houseplants will tell you when they need to be re-potted in a couple of ways. The most common way is during watering. When there are more roots than soil in the pot, water will drain quickly out of the bottom. Other signals... the plant becomes top heavy tipping over easily, roots are growing out the bottom or top of the container, or the container breaks from the pressure of roots pushing out." "Plants should be potted into bigger containers in no more than a 2-3" increase in diameter size from the original pot. Plants put into 'too big' of a container can be susceptible to root rot." "Make sure to pot houseplants into a container that has a drainage hole". [A51]
- UMD (pot-bound): "Pot-bound plants have tight masses of roots that often fill the pot and sometimes come out of the pot over the edge." "Often when watering a pot-bound plant the water passes through the pot too quickly and isn't absorbed adequately so that the center of the root ball remains dry." Management: "repotted into larger pots and the tight root mass should be loosened by hand or cut away". [A10]
- Clemson: "A pot-bound plant has filled its pot with roots. There is not enough soil to hold the water for all those roots, so the plant wilts. Correct this problem by repotting in a larger pot." [A17]; ISU: "If the container is too small and plants are rootbound, repot in a larger container." [A26]
- UC IPM: pot-bound → hojas viejas amarillas y caída/marchitez (tablas) [A1].
- Advertencias contra el tiesto excesivo: MOBOT "too large a pot" → pudrición [A32]; RHS "overpotting" → amarilleo [A28]; ISU "The container is too large, and the large volume of soil takes too long to dry out" [A21]; Penn State: raíces que asoman por la **superficie** tienen otras causas: "Roots are very close to the soil surface: Plant grown on too hot a surface (TV, radiator); Soil kept too wet; Soil too compact; Only enough water is added to the wet top layer; Soil washed away during watering" [A19].

**Cómo diferenciar**: raíces por el drenaje + agua que atraviesa en segundos + necesidad de riego muy frecuente + hojas bajas amarillas + planta que vuelca → pot-bound (WSU, Montana, UMN, UMD); raíces en la **superficie** → riego superficial, sustrato compacto/encharcado o calor por debajo (Penn State); unas pocas raíces por el agujero en planta vigorosa, sin los demás signos → no urgente (Montana: cortarlas al trasplantar).

**Preguntas**: ¿cuánto tarda en salir el agua? ¿cada cuánto riega? ¿vuelca? ¿cuándo fue el último trasplante? ¿hojas bajas amarillas? ¿costra de sales?

**Tratamiento (menor a mayor)**: 1) regar a fondo por inmersión si el centro queda seco (UMD, UMD invierno); 2) trasplantar a un tiesto solo 2,5-5 cm (WSU) / 5-7,5 cm (UMN) / un tercio (Montana) mayor, con drenaje, en final de invierno/primavera o cuando haya estrés (Montana, UMN "any time of the year"); 3) aflojar o cortar la masa radicular (UMD, Montana); 4) en plantas que no se quieren agrandar, renovar sustrato sin subir de tiesto (Ask Extension/UMD: "Repotting doesn't necessarily have to move a plant into a larger pot" [A54]).

**Recuperación y descarte**: Montana: amarilleo/caída normal tras trasplante; sin abono ≥2 semanas; luz indirecta unos días [A52]. No se descarta.

**Urgencia**: Baja.

**Fuentes**: A1, A10, A17, A19, A21, A26, A28, A32, A47, A51, A52, A54.

## B. Protocolo fotográfico

Lo que piden las clínicas de diagnóstico y los servicios "Ask Extension" cuando el diagnóstico se hace por foto. Ninguna de las fuentes abiertas es específica de plantas de interior; se citan tal cual y se señala la adaptación a maceta.

### B.1 Qué fotos (conjunto mínimo)

- **Tres vistas** es el patrón común. Illinois (clínica, 2003 y 2007): "Three good pictures are usually enough. Pictures should show the overall pattern in the landscape, the pattern on one plant, and a close-up of the symptoms."; "Please dont send more pictures because these take a long time to download and generally dont add much information." [B6][B7]. Mississippi State P3022: "submit a series of photos that show multiple views: The location of the symptomatic plant(s) and the area surrounding the plant(s) (a 'field view'). The entire plant (a 'plant view'). A close-up of any symptomatic plant parts (e.g., an entire leaf or fruit). A close-up of any signs or symptoms (e.g., fungal survival structures... or an area that shows stem anomalies or several leaf spots/lesions)." Y por qué: "'Field views' and 'plant views' can show patterns of symptoms that are important to diagnosis. Examples of patterns include the pattern of chlorotic leaves on a plant". [B5]. Virginia Tech: "Include ~3 to a maximum of 7 well-focused images that show: a. the pattern of the problem in the location... b. the overall symptoms on the whole plant c. a close-up of the symptoms on an affected plant, including images of both leaf surfaces if there are symptoms on leaves" [B10]. Delaware: "Three to four well-focused, quality images that show the pattern of the problem in the location, the overall symptoms on the whole plant and a close-up image of the symptoms" [B11]. CSU Boulder: "Take several pictures (digital film is free!) but send only the best three or four (limit four)." [B9]. SDSU: "We only need two or three good images. A close-up photo of dying or affected plant tissue, including the border between healthy and affected tissue. A picture of the whole plant. A picture of the surrounding area." [B13]. Cornell: "Plant diseases and mystery problems are almost always 3-5 photos." [B8]. Wisconsin PDDC, en cambio: "Err on the side of taking too many photos." [B15].
- **Planta entera "de la tierra a la copa"**: Illinois "photograph the entire plant from the soil line to the top of the plant" [B6]; K-State "Take photos of the entire plant that includes all plant parts from soil level to the top of the plant. Ensure the symptoms are still visible in this type of image." [B12]; Wisconsin "Whole plant shots... will show the distribution of symptoms on the plant. Are the symptoms in just one area? Are they scattered throughout the plant? Is the entire plant affected?" [B15].
- **Entorno** (en interior: ventana, radiador, distancia, otras plantas): K-State "Stand back and capture the entire landscape where the plant resides. Include in the photo the surrounding plants, concrete, rocks, drain spouts, or whatever else is near the symptomatic plant. Don't worry if the symptoms cannot be seen in this type of image... This gives us look at how the affected plant is growing within the site and if there are any site issues that might be contributing to the problem." [B12]; Wisconsin "They can often provide clues on environmental factors that may be contributing to the disease problem" [B15].
- **Hoja afectada por haz y envés**: Virginia Tech (arriba); Clemson HGIC 2368 "For leaves, take photos of the top AND bottom of leaves. Pests like mites or aphids may not be evident on the tops of leaves but obvious on the lower surface." [B3]; UMD/UVM "Take photos of both the top and bottom of leaves. Some insects cause damage only visible on the leaf undersides." [B1][B2]; NC State "If there is a problem on a leaf, take photos of both the top and the underside of the leaf." [B4]; Cornell "disease symptoms can vary from the upper and lower sides of leaves and images of both can be helpful" [B8]; UMN "For foliar disease symptoms, take photos from both the top and bottom leaf surfaces. Sometimes getting photos of leaf tissue that is backlit (the leaf is situated between your camera and the sun) can reveal important clues." [B14]; Wisconsin "affected leaves (both tops and bottoms)" [B15].
- **Transición sano-enfermo**: NC State "If possible, get an image of healthy plant tissue along with damaged plant parts." [B4]; SDSU "including the border between healthy and affected tissue" [B13]; Cornell "the portion of the plant showing symptoms but not yet fully destroyed, and a focused close up of the symptom"; "a mid-aged leaf that is starting to yellow and is showing lesions" [B8]; K-State "Send a 'healthy' plant and a 'sick' plant" (muestra física) [B12]; UMN "Ignore the dead plants (photograph the sick, but alive ones)" [B14]; WSU "Send specimens showing diseased and healthy tissue"; "Please do not submit dead, dry, decayed or rotted samples" [B16].
- **Base del tallo / cuello**: Illinois "Pull back foliage from around the plant base and take a picture of the base as it enters the soil." [B6][B7]; Cornell "a close-up of a sad plant focused at the lower stem" [B8]; UMD/UVM "Inspect the stems, trunk, the ground/soil, and the leaves. Take and send a photo even if you are not sure if it is relevant." [B1][B2]; Clemson "inspect the trunk of the tree and ground for signs of fungi or insects" [B3].
- **Raíces y sustrato** (en maceta: sacar la planta del tiesto): Cornell "For diseases causing root symptoms, abiotic issues, or mysteries take a picture of the field..., a whole plant above-ground photo, pictures any above ground symptoms on the foliage, a shot of what the roots look like, and an image of the crown sliced open vertically." [B8]; UMN "Sometimes there can be foliar symptoms caused by pathogens that remain in root or stem tissue... splitting stems, stalks and roots can provide valuable clues" [B14]; WSU "Many problems originate below ground in and around roots—include roots and soil for more accurate diagnosis!" [B16]; UC IPM "Houseplants can often be removed from their containers so that roots can be examined" e "Inspect root systems whenever wilting occurs" [A1]; RHS "Tip the plant gently out of its pot and check the condition of the roots" [A28].
- **Maceta**: ninguna fuente de clínica lo pide explícitamente (NO ENCONTRADO como instrucción fotográfica), pero los síntomas que requieren verla están documentados: costra en el borde y el agujero de drenaje (Clemson [A18], UMD [A7]), verdín en barro (WSU [A47]), ausencia de drenaje (ISU [A21]), agua en el plato (UMD [A5]). K-State pide fotografiar "the entire plant... from soil level", lo que en maceta incluye el tiesto [B12].
- **Insectos**: UMD/UVM "Crumpled insects without an indicator of size makes ID very difficult... Take top-down, underside, and side views of the insect if possible. Capture flying or crawling insects in a container and put them in the freezer for 15 minutes" [B1][B2]; NC State "look for both mature and immature forms. Close-ups of legs and antennae can be helpful. Images of the damage... Frass or casings" [B4]; Cornell: foto a través de una lupa de mano para hongos y bichos diminutos ("Hold the scouting lens up to the camera lens... make sure the subject is in the center") [B8].

### B.2 Condiciones de la foto

- **Enfoque**: Illinois "Focus and lighting are the two most frequent problems... Hold the camera still and squeeze the shutter-release button rather than moving the whole camera"; "If they are blurred, dont send them." [B6][B7]; Clemson "Make sure photos are in clear focus. Take multiple photos and choose the sharpest to send." [B3]; CSU "To focus a cell phone camera, often times simply tapping the screen will adjust the focus automatically" [B9]; K-State "Ensure the image is in focus by tapping the image before you take the shot." [B12]; Cornell "Stabilize the camera. Prop your elbow on your knee, ribs, the ground...", "Center the focal point on the subject", "Try using portrait mode on your phone. Or, put your hand directly behind the subject to obscure the background and refocus" [B8]; UMD/UVM "Wipe off your cell phone camera lens before taking the photos as it is common for skin oil to accumulate and fog up the picture." [B1][B2]; Cornell "Clean off the lens"; "Clean up the area around the subject. Push unwanted leaves out of the way" [B8].
- **Distancia vs. zoom**: Clemson "It is generally better to get as close to the object being photographed rather than zoom in from a distance. The higher the degree of the zoom, the lower the resolution... The automatic focus feature of cell phone cameras may have problems focusing on the subject if the camera is held too closely. In these cases, you may get better results backing out about 6 inches and then using the zoom feature." [B3]; UMD/UVM "move your phone as close to the subject as possible rather than relying heavily on the digital zoom. However, once within a foot or so of a small subject, your camera may not be able to focus that close. Consider backing up a bit and enlarging with a small amount of digital zoom" [B1][B2]; Cornell "Zooming in too far blurs an image. Better to adjust your distance from the subject first, then zoom" [B8]; NC State "Back out until you can get a shot in focus." [B4].
- **Luz natural, sin contraluz**: UMN "Try to take photos using good natural lighting. Bringing plant samples back to the office also brings them out of sunlight and results in poor picture quality." [B14]; UMD/UVM "Avoid taking photos with the sun or major light sources in front of you, as your camera will adjust and make your subject dark. Move your camera or the subject so that the light is behind you... In harsh sunlight conditions, shading your subject may provide better lighting and clarity." [B1][B2]; Illinois "Always work with the sun or major source of light behind you. With the light source behind the subject, the image is dark, looks like a shadow, and masks details" [B6]; Clemson "Try not to take photos facing the sun as it may wash out the photo" [B3]; UMD/UVM insectos "Photos need to be in focus and taken in good, natural light." [B1]; Virginia Tech "Try to avoid strong shadows on the sample; outdoor images taken on a cloudy day are best. When taking pictures indoors, try to illuminate the plant from both sides to eliminate shadows in the photo and place on a neutral background." [B10]; CSU "Make sure that the features of the plant or the plant disease are in full light, as shadows can obscure the specimen" [B9]; Cornell "On bright days, hold your ball cap bill over the phone to shade the lens & reduce oversaturation"; "Adjust the lighting (white balance) of your image before taking the photo!... Make the colors match what your eye sees." [B8].
- **Flash**: NO ENCONTRADO ninguna instrucción explícita sobre usar o no flash en las fuentes abiertas (Virginia Tech pide iluminar "from both sides" en interior; Cornell y UMD piden fidelidad de color).
- **Fondo neutro**: Virginia Tech "place on a neutral background" [B10]; Cornell "Place on any non-reflective surface like the ground, a truck seat, or even your pant leg"; "Clean up the area around the subject... This will help the camera isolate the subject from the background" [B8].
- **Fidelidad de color / sin filtros**: UMD "Disable any artistic cell phone camera filters that change the image's colors or appearance." [B1]; Cornell "The image matches exactly what you are seeing. Same colors, same level of detail."; "Take off your sunglasses so you can match the image exactly to what you see." [B8].
- **Objeto de escala**: CSU "Include a size reference. Depending on the size, place a coin, pencil, ruler, or yardstick next to the specimen" [B9]; UMD/UVM "include an object in the photo with your subject to provide sizing context, such as a ruler (the best option), coin, pen or pencil, battery, or a finger." [B1][B2]; Clemson "A ruler or tape measure is ideal, but an object like a coin, pen, key, or another common household item can be used." [B3]; NC State "Use an object to set the scale. Examples are coins, pens, or rulers." [B4]; SDSU "with a penny or other item next to it for scale" [B13].
- **Resolución y formato**: Virginia Tech "All images must be in GIF, JPG, PNG, or PDF, but we prefer GIF or JPG. Do not send small-size images (e.g. < 500 pixels); ~2,000 pixels is great." [B10]; Wisconsin "High resolution photos. The higher the resolution, the better I will be able to increase the size of the picture and still see lots of detail." [B15]; SDSU "the smaller the organism or issue in question, the higher the resolution needed... Please do not send videos." [B13].
- **Varios ángulos y varias tomas**: UMD/UVM "Take photos from multiple angles." [B1]; Clemson "Try to get photos from different angles and distances" [B3]; NC State "Take multiple shots." [B4].

### B.3 Datos que deben acompañar a las fotos

- Mississippi State: "please do not send photos without additional information. Photos alone can provide valuable information, but the combination of photos and information, such as the variety of the affected crop and descriptions of the symptoms together, can be monumental in comparison." [B5]
- UMD/UVM: "Did something change in the plant's environment before you noticed symptoms?... How long has the issue been going on? Was there a notable weather event...? Where in the landscape did you find the issue?... very sunny or very shady area?" [B1][B2]; Clemson: "Did the problem appear after a severe weather event...? Where was the specimen found...? How long has the problem been occurring/when did it first appear...? Has there been any human activity just prior to the problem (e.g., I started noticing a problem after I applied herbicide...)?" [B3].
- K-State ("Useful background information"): "Site history... Irrigation practices: Frequency of irrigation, length of time, irrigation application method..., time of day. Chemical history: Pesticide usage and timing, fertilizer applications, etc. Pattern on plant: Describe the problem. Are symptoms on new or old growth? Top or bottom of plant? Pattern in landscape: One host or multiple hosts? Other plants in the landscape showing similar symptoms? Timing: When did the symptoms occur: All at once? (i.e. after a storm?) Slowly over time?" [B12]
- SDSU: "The variety or hybrid of the plant, if known. The planting date or age of the plant. Watering method and how often the plant is being watered. As well as the fertilizers type and amount that has been applied. If any pesticides have been sprayed or used in the area". [B13]
- CSU: "where it is growing, when symptoms were noticed, how many plants are affected, and any other relevant details." [B9]

### C.2 Indicadores que comparar entre visitas

- **Patrón y progresión** (UMD): "Noticing the pattern and progression of symptoms will help to diagnose the cause"; "If the stress-causing condition is not alleviated, yellow leaves may turn brown"; "A prolonged period of environmental stress causes overall stunting and poor growth." [A4]
- **Humedad del sustrato y peso del tiesto**: UMD "As plants use water, the potting mix will dry out and become lighter in weight. Periodically lift the pot for changes in weight and compare to when it was watered. On large containers insert a stick or a dowel... Moist soil will stick to the dowel or discolor it slightly." [A5][A15]; UMD invierno: "Practice your skills by holding pots just after thorough watering... Then repeat the hand weighing process over the course of the next week"; "on small pots check your estimates after a week by carefully releasing the plant from its pot and inspecting the root system visually"; objetivo invernal "like a freshly wrung out dish towel" [A11]; MOBOT "lift the pot. If it is light, it is in need of water" [A32]; ISU palillo [A27]; OSU "inserting a finger into the soil to the second knuckle, or lift the pot" [A41]; ISU "dry down an inch or two" [A22].
- **Raíces**: color (Illinois blanco/naranja-marrón/negro [A38]; UC IPM "Healthy roots should be white" [A1]; UMD "light-colored and firm" [A3]).
- **Nuevo crecimiento** como señal de recuperación: RHS "Retain leaves that are predominantly green until the plant has produced a fresh flush of leaves" [A29]; UConn "when new growth is produced, some of the more spindly stems can usually be pruned out" [A39]; ISU "once the issue is fixed, new growth will look better" [A21].
- **Color, turgencia, tamaño de hoja y flores**: Penn State (clave): hojas pequeñas, pálidas, entrenudos [A19]; WSU C064 (tabla síntoma-causa) [A47]; MOBOT "off color", "slight drooping" como primeros avisos de sequía [A32].
- **Plagas**: UC IPM lista de lugares a revisar: "leaf surfaces, leaf bases, flower and fruit stalks, and roots"; "in tight crevices on the plant, such as behind or inside leaf bases, within leaflet folds, or in flower or fruit clusters" [A1]; UMN papel blanco [A48]; melaza en mesa/suelo (Arkansas, MOBOT) [A42][A32]; mosquitos al regar (OSU) [A40].
- **Sustrato y maceta**: costra blanca, verdín, moho, olor (Clemson, WSU, UMD, OSU) [A17][A47][A16][A40]; agua que atraviesa demasiado rápido (UMN, UMD) [A51][A10].
- **Sales**: lavado programado "every 4 to 6 months" (UMD) [A7].

### C.3 Señales de deterioro temprano

- UMD: "Leaf yellowing is often one of the first symptoms of plant stress." [A4]
- MOBOT: "Wilting leaves, slight drooping or an off color can signal a plant is getting too dry"; "Leaf and flower drop... It is a very common initial reaction to a stress placed on the plant." [A32]
- MOBOT FAQ: "Learn to recognize such symptoms early, make correlations with environmental conditions, and take corrective steps at the earliest possible time." [A33]
- OSU (Sanchez): "most important is careful observation of the plants to see how they are responding, mindful that there is sometimes a delay between an event that affects a plant and evidence of that event." [A40]
- UMN: araña roja "hard to spot until their populations become large enough to see webbing" → de ahí la prueba del papel [A48].
- MSU 1987: "Many people who care for houseplants do not recognize problems until one or more plants have been severely damaged." [A46]
- UC IPM: raíces "Off-color (brownish to blackish, or dark yellowish) roots, especially at the root tips, often indicate that root rot is present." [A1]
- ISU: "Treating symptoms like fungus gnats with insecticide won't help if the root cause, such as wet soil, isn't addressed." [A20]
- Plazos de referencia para la app (de A): aclimatación ≤ 3 semanas (Clemson); amarilleo que dura "longer than a few weeks" tras mudanza = cambiar de sitio (Illinois); frío: síntomas hasta 1 semana después (UMD); tras corregir exceso de riego: 2 semanas → abono, 2 semanas más → trasplante (Illinois); tras trasplante: sin abono ≥ 2 semanas (Montana); inspección semanal de plantas nuevas y mensual después (UC IPM); cuarentena "a couple of weeks" (UMN).
